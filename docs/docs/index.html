Record, edit and deliver video on your own computer.

Reccut brings screen and camera recording, a magnetic timeline, multicam, audio cleanup, colour, titles, an AI Agent and export into one app. Your projects stay on your computer, and AI models are downloaded only when you ask for them. Start with a guided first edit, or jump to the part of Reccut you want to learn.

Third-party Software and Licenses

Find offline license texts, component credits and information about source-code and artwork rights.

On this pageSeparate component licensesYour recordings and artwork

Open About Reccut → Third-party licenses to read the local component list and license texts. On macOS, About Reccut is in the Reccut menu. On Windows and Linux, use the Help menu. The license reader works offline; project links open upstream websites in your browser.

Choose Open license files and documentation in the reader to open the local notices directory. DISTRIBUTION.md contains more detail about source-code and distribution requirements. The directory also contains original license texts and a component manifest with the versions recorded at build time.

Separate component licenses

Reccut uses Electron, Preact, Signals, Immer, Lucide, Tabler, PDF.js, Mediabunny, FFmpeg, uiohook-napi, Transformers.js and ONNX Runtime, along with model weights and artwork. Each component retains its own license and copyright notices. The application license does not replace those terms.

FFmpeg is used as a separate command-line program under GPLv3 or later. Mediabunny is MPL 2.0. The MIT-licensed uiohook-napi wrapper contains the LGPL-licensed libuiohook native library. These licenses include source-code and distribution rights, beyond the notices shown in About.

Whisper and the BiRefNet models, which are downloaded when you ask for them, declare MIT licenses. OpenMoji graphics use CC BY-SA 4.0, and Unicode emoji data has its own license. When included, Blackmagic RAW libraries have separate proprietary SDK terms.

Your recordings and artwork

Processing a recording with FFmpeg does not by itself make the recording GPL-licensed. Imported footage, fonts, stock images and artwork still have their own terms. Preserve attribution and license information for assets such as OpenMoji graphics and downloaded stock images. Copy Credits in Design's export options can help collect the source information stored with supported assets.

For complete terms, use the original texts in the license reader. For build-specific source delivery and native-library requirements, read DISTRIBUTION.md in the local license folder.

Design Thumbnails and Images

Make layered images from video frames, photos, text and shapes, then export or package them.

Open Design in the title bar. Choose New Design for a size preset or enter a custom size from 1 to 8192 pixels. You can keep several designs in a project. The bottom strip shows them as pages: click to open, double click to rename, or right click to duplicate, make a variant or delete. The + button adds a design, and Export opens the export choices.

The left rail opens Search, Templates, Video, Text, Add Content, Elements, Styles or Brand. Only one panel is open at a time. Click its rail icon again or Close panel to give the canvas more room. Expand panel widens it. The last panel and width are remembered. Layers is above Properties on the right. Dragging a layer opens an animated gap; move to another position before dropping. Groups move together. Escape or dropping outside cancels; only a completed drop adds an Undo step. The list scrolls near its edges.

Add pictures

Open Add Content and choose Import Image. Its main action follows the import copy preference. The arrow menu offers Import Embedded, Import Linked and Open as New Design. Import Embedded copies a photo into Designs/Assets. For a layered PSD, PSB or SVG, choose Place as one image or Place layers. Select the layers to keep. Layer positions and order are preserved, and imported layers are grouped. Import Linked references the original file. The Design menu also keeps Place Embedded and Place Linked. Design pictures stay separate from the Edit media browser. Recent shows images imported into this project; From Project references images already imported in Edit.

The Video panel has its own preview and scrubber. Use This Frame saves a full quality PNG of the sequence or selected source video into Designs/Frames. The full frame is placed with its original aspect ratio, fitted inside the design without cropping. Move and resize the layer to compose the thumbnail. Changing the timeline afterwards does not change this image. Click the timecode to type HH:MM:SS:FF, HH:MM:SS, MM:SS or seconds, then press Enter or click away to seek. Escape cancels; invalid entries keep the previous position. After Enter, arrow keys are ready to step frames in the Video panel. Play/Pause and Space control this preview independently of Edit. The side buttons skip five seconds; hold Shift to skip ten. Arrow keys step one frame while the Video panel has focus; Shift and arrows step one second. Typing, scrubbing or stepping pauses playback. While typing a timecode, arrow keys move the text caret. Navigation stops at the first and last visible frame. Use This Frame can also create the first design. An empty or audio only timeline cannot supply a video frame.

Drop files to place them using the import copy preference. Hold Alt while dropping to reverse the choice. Cmd/Ctrl V pastes an image from the system clipboard. Cmd/Ctrl C and X copy and cut selected layers, and V pastes them into another design or project. Referenced images travel with the layers; embedded files are copied into the receiving project. Plain text becomes a text layer. Paste keeps relative centers when the design size changes. Shift Cmd/Ctrl V pastes in place. Linked images have a link icon. A missing image has a warning in Layers: click Relink to find the file before exporting.

Edit layers

V selects the Move Tool, T the Type Tool, U the Shape Tool, H the Hand Tool and C the Crop Tool. Shift U cycles rectangle, ellipse, polygon, star, line and arrow. The shape menu also offers each tool. Double click text to edit it directly; Escape finishes editing. For text, Character appears first in Properties with searchable installed fonts, font refresh, weight, size, Tracking, Leading, All Caps and Text Color. Download and install a Google Font on your computer to use it here; Browse Google Fonts opens the font site. Select words in Content or while editing on the canvas to change just their font, size, weight, tracking, case or color. The selected-text notice identifies the words being formatted; Whole layer clears that selection. Position, paragraph alignment, masks and other layer controls continue to affect the layer. Rich text is retained when saving, exporting and copying layers. Fit text in box shrinks long titles, down to 6 pixels. At the minimum size, extremely long text can still be clipped.

Click or Shift click to select layers, or drag on empty canvas to make a selection box. Drag the selection to move it; guides snap to edges and centers. Hold Cmd/Ctrl to turn snapping off. With Rulers on, drag from the top or left ruler to pull out a guide. Layers snap to it, the Move Tool drags it, and dragging it back to a ruler removes it. Guides are never exported and hide with the rulers. Arrow keys move one pixel; Shift and arrows move ten. Backspace deletes; Cmd/Ctrl J duplicates a layer. Cmd/Ctrl D or Escape deselects. Cmd/Ctrl T selects Free Transform.

Drag handles to resize and use the handle above the box to rotate. Images keep their aspect ratio by default; Shift frees it. For other layers Shift locks aspect. Alt resizes from the center. Shift snaps rotation to 15 degrees. Space and drag, or scrolling, pans. Cmd/Ctrl and scrolling zooms, Cmd/Ctrl 0 fits on screen. Use the 100% command for actual size. Cmd/Ctrl 1 still opens Record, so 100% has no shortcut.

Layers are shown top first. Drag the divider above Properties to change the Layers height; the app remembers it. Double click the divider to return to the compact automatic height. The divider also supports Up/Down, Home and End keys. Drag rows to change their order; use eye and lock buttons to hide or protect a layer. Double click a layer name to rename it. Right click for more layer actions. Selecting a locked layer lets you unlock it, but does not allow transforms.

Styles and text

Properties has Fill controls for solid colors and linear, radial or freeform gradients. The gradient spans the layer box and follows its rotation. Stroke offers Size, Position (Inside, Center, Outside), solid/dashed/dotted styles and joins. Text starts with an Outside stroke. A Size of zero hides the stroke.

Layer Styles adds Soft Drop Shadow, Hard Shadow, Outer Glow or Long Shadow. You can add several shadows, reorder them and adjust Color, Opacity, Angle, Distance, Spread and Size. Spread also follows the transparency of images. Blend Mode controls how a layer combines with the layers below it. Images also have Brightness, Contrast, Saturation, Hue, Blur and Grayscale, with Reset Adjustments.

Use paired asterisks, *words*, to give words the Highlight fill. Type \* to keep a literal asterisk. Consecutive or unmatched asterisks remain literal. Text Effects can add a padded, rounded background box behind each line. Missing local fonts fall back to the system font and show Font not installed in Properties and Layers.

Groups, alignment and crop

Cmd/Ctrl G groups selected layers and Shift Cmd/Ctrl G ungroups them. A click selects the group; double click selects a member. In Layers, Alt click also selects one member. Collapse a group with its arrow and double click its name to rename it. Locked group members must be unlocked before moving, regrouping or changing group order. Group resize preserves each member's rotation and does not add skew.

Align and Distribute in Properties aligns selected bounds. With one layer, alignment uses the canvas. Distribution needs at least three layers and makes the gaps equal. Rotated layers use their visible rectangular bounds.

Double click an image or choose Crop… in Properties to show the full source dimmed around the crop. This also works on locked image layers. Drag the image to reposition it and drag handles to change the crop. Hold Shift to keep the crop's shape, or Alt to scale it from the center, also with Free. Choose Free, Design, 1:1, 16:9, 9:16 or 4:5. Enter or Apply Crop applies it in one undo step; Escape or Cancel discards it. Reset Crop restores the full source within the current layer box.

Design shortcuts

  • V Move Tool, T Type Tool, U Shape Tool.
  • Shift+U Next Shape Tool, H Hand Tool, C Crop Tool.
  • Ctrl+G Group Layers, Ctrl+Shift+G Ungroup Layers.
  • Ctrl+J Duplicate Layer, Ctrl+D Deselect, Ctrl+T Free Transform.
  • Ctrl+Shift+V Paste in Place.
  • Ctrl+] Bring Forward, Ctrl+[ Send Backward, Ctrl+Shift+] Bring to Front, Ctrl+Shift+[ Send to Back.
  • ← / → / ↑ / ↓ move one pixel. Shift moves ten pixels.
  • Ctrl++ Zoom In, Ctrl+- Zoom Out, Ctrl+0 Fit on Screen.
  • Ctrl+Alt+Shift+W Export As.

Settings has a separate Design keyboard group. Shortcut conflicts are checked within the current room and against shared commands such as Save. Quick Search lists every Design command, including alignment and distribution.

Save and export

Canvas Size changes the canvas without scaling layers. Use its nine-point content anchor to choose where existing content stays. Resize scales content for new sizes.

Design changes save with the project and have their own Undo and Redo. Undo in Design keeps the timeline intact, and Undo in Edit keeps designs intact. Opening a project clears both histories.

Export As saves PNG, JPEG or WebP using the same drawing as the canvas. PNG and WebP can omit the canvas background for transparency. JPEG uses an opaque background. Choose a width or scale and an optional file size limit in MB. Zero disables the limit. If a file cannot fit, reduce its width or raise the limit. Existing files get numbered names. Quick Export as PNG uses the current design size and canvas background, retaining transparency when the canvas has no background.

Package Project includes design assets and image media used by designs, even when Remove unused media is enabled. Fonts are listed in the package report and must be installed on the destination computer.

Color correction

Select images, text, shapes or a mixture of layers and expand Color Correction in Properties. Light offers Exposure in stops, Brightness, Contrast, Highlights and Shadows. Color offers Hue, Saturation, Temperature and Tint. Curves has RGB, Red, Green and Blue channels: click to add points, drag to adjust, use arrow keys to fine-tune and Delete to remove a selected interior point. Effects has Blur and Grayscale. These panels collapse independently. Enable compares with the original appearance; Reset All clears the corrections. Multi-selection changes only the setting being edited, preserving each layer's other corrections. Edits can be undone, stay editable after saving, and use the same rendering for the canvas and export. Saved layer styles include color corrections.

Cutouts and masks

Select an image and choose Remove Background in Properties. The model runs locally with the app, including without internet. Tasks shows progress and Cancel. The original image stays intact; the result is a separate mask. Feather softens the edge and Shift Edge expands or contracts it. Edit Layer Mask opens Erase and Restore brushes with Size and Hardness. Each stroke can be undone and redone. Done leaves brush mode. Sticker Outline follows the final visible edge; shadows follow it too.

Layer Mask adds a rectangle, ellipse or polygon. Edit Mask shows that shape on the canvas with draggable handles, including the ellipse outline. Edit Mask moves its handles; Feather and Invert adjust its edge. Clip to Layer Below keeps a layer inside the visible layer below. Select one text layer and one image, then Fill Text With Image to make a clipping group. The Video panel offers Cut Out Camera when the selected recording has a camera stream. Cut Out This Frame captures the selected full frame and removes its background locally, keeping the original source and a separate editable mask.

Export Design is the blue button at the top right. Copy Credits in Export As copies attribution for third-party images. Settings → Design controls online sources, sign-in and the optional online template catalog. Search terms go only to enabled sources; downloaded images stay in the project. Design → Plugins → Image Source Plugins offers optional sources such as Pixabay with your own API key. Add the source, get a key on its website, save it locally and use Test Connection. Source exports and projects never include keys. Pexels still needs app approval; Unsplash is not offered as a personal-key plugin.

Reusable styles and templates

The Text panel offers Add Title, Add Subtitle and Add Badge, sized for the canvas and filled with the project name. Brand Fonts supplies the title and body fonts and changes matching existing text when edited. Brand also stores color roles, an emoji style and named Brand Kits; new generic elements use these defaults. Apply Brand restyles existing editable elements. Explicit library styles retain their example palette. Font Pairs applies separate title and subtitle fonts together; pairs with missing local fonts are hidden. It also offers 111 built-in text styles and My Styles. Save Style in the section header stores the selected layer's appearance. Text size adapts to the design height. Right click a saved style to rename, update from the selection, or delete it. You can store up to 500 styles.

Text styles with a second text color automatically emphasize the longest word using the same colors as their example. Equal-length words use the first one. Existing words marked with paired asterisks keep their emphasis instead. Applying a style replaces prior character overrides for that style's appearance settings. Your text and layer placement stay intact, and Undo restores the previous formatting. You can select another word in Properties to customize its color afterward.

Library sections show a horizontal row of cards. See All opens the whole section as a grid; Back returns to the sections. Search filters the current panel. Hover or focus previews a card on the canvas without saving or adding undo steps. A single click applies the card and selects it; Enter also applies it. Double clicking inserts only once. Drag an image to place it at that point. Drag a style or color onto a compatible unlocked layer to affect only that layer. Right click offers Apply and the actions available for that item.

Templates offers 22 built-in layouts for wide, tall and square designs, plus My Templates. Click a template or press Enter to create a new design using its size or the current canvas size. It uses the current frame, cutout, project title and My Logo when available. By Style groups templates by tags such as Bold, Minimal, Tutorial and News. Save as Template lets you assign picture and text placeholders, comma-separated tags and a color role for each text or shape layer. Import and export use .reccut-design-template files, without links to original project files.

Brand has the reusable Logo, Brand Colors, title/body Brand Fonts and a Brand Palette. Click a color to apply it to selected text or shapes, or to the background when nothing is selected. My Colors also stores up to 20 colors beside color controls. Save Color in Brand adds the chosen color; right click a swatch to remove it. My Logo is copied into each new design that uses it, so replacing the saved logo keeps existing projects intact.

Elements, palettes and search

Elements offers editable shapes and badges, hand-drawn arrows, circles and underlines, system emoji, stickers, solid/gradient backgrounds and textures. Click or press Enter to add an item; drag places it at that canvas point. Badges remain text layers, including the sequence duration badge. Backgrounds, including gradients, create separate editable layers. New backgrounds are placed above older background layers and below normal content. Dots and grids have Pattern controls for background and foreground colors, symbol size or line width, and independent X/Y spacing. Symbols include circles, squares, diamonds, triangles, stars and hexagons. Size Variation randomizes their sizes; Random Seed selects a repeatable arrangement. Size Distribution makes symbols or grid lines larger in the center, at the edges, or toward the left, right, top or bottom. Distribution Strength controls how strongly sizes change. Older raster textures offer Make Pattern Editable, which can be undone. Right click an element to save it to My Elements. Emoji offers 3,781 choices, English search, categories and paged grids. Brand selects System, Flat or Outline as its default; the library follows this choice and Properties can change one emoji. Flat/Outline use bundled OpenMoji graphics with CC BY-SA 4.0 credits; their appearance depends on the installed system fonts.

Styles has Recommended, Colors and Fonts. Recommended combines a five-color palette with a font pair. Colors applies palettes alone; From This Frame samples colors locally from the frame selected in Video, or a suggested sequence frame if none was selected. Brand Palette uses your saved colors. Font pairs use installed fonts only. Right click to save palettes or font pairs for reuse, or delete saved items.

Palettes map to background, title, highlight, accent and stroke roles. For layers without roles, the first title uses the title color, other text uses accent and shapes use highlight. Applying a palette preserves photos, hidden layers and locked layers or groups. Dropping a palette on compatible text or a shape changes only that layer and keeps the background intact. Hover previews the result before applying it; Undo restores the previous appearance.

Search in the rail finds built-in and saved templates, text styles, elements, palettes, font pairs, project images, brand colors and your logo. Results are grouped by type. Exact names come first, followed by names starting with the search words. See All opens a group's grid. Search works offline.

Design suggestions

Templates shows For This Video as cards after local analysis. Video shows Best Frames from the same analysis. Hover previews a suggestion; click or Enter captures the full-quality original. Templates creates an editable layout; Best Frames places a background image, preserving the complete frame and its aspect ratio. Either can create the first design. Changing the timeline discards stale frame suggestions. Title and Shuffle in Templates let you adjust the inline suggestions. More Suggestions opens the dialog with additional sizes, title ideas and optional AI title suggestions.

Choose Suggestions from the Design empty state, the Templates panel or the Design menu. Reccut analyzes the current sequence locally and offers up to six frames with four layouts for each. It favors clear, well-exposed frames, avoids fades and transitions, and spreads choices across the video. Camera visibility adds priority. Analysis is limited to about 14 seconds; slow or unavailable frames can be skipped.

Choose a canvas size and title, or type your own title. Offline title ideas come from the project and sequence names, timeline titles and captions, including Norwegian captions. Shuffle changes the layouts and title. Click a card to capture that frame at full quality and create a new editable design in one undo step. The entire source frame is retained without automatic cropping. The timeline and its playhead stay unchanged.

When an AI provider is configured, Suggest Titles with AI is available for sequences with captions. The first use in a Suggestions window names the provider and asks before sending up to 12,000 transcript characters. No image, audio or video file is sent by this action. AI never runs automatically, and offline suggestions remain available if the provider fails.

Closing Suggestions cancels the analysis or pending frame capture. Switching projects, rooms or changing the sequence also discards stale results.

Preview and readability

Preview in the toolbar shows generic video-list layouts at 360, 246 and 168 pixels wide and in a phone layout. Switch light/dark backgrounds and the progress bar. The duration badge uses the current sequence duration. The whole design is fitted inside each preview.

Safe Zones highlights generic overlay areas for the selected size on the editing canvas. It also draws a safe frame that follows the shape of the canvas: action safe at 90%, title safe at 80%, a center cross and lines for a 4:3 center crop on wide or tall designs. These areas are guides, not platform requirements, and are never exported. Readability in the toolbar automatically checks for small text, low text-fill contrast and text overlapping an overlay area after edits. A warning dot appears when there are issues. Open it to review the warnings or run Check Readability again. Select jumps to the affected text layer. Checks use the layers behind the text and a white backdrop for transparency. Outlines and shadows may improve actual legibility.

Variants and sizes

Canvas offers Make Variant. Export in the bottom strip offers Compare Variants. Each variant is independently editable. Keep this one marks a favorite without deleting any design. Favorites appear in the designs strip.

Resize in the toolbar creates designs for one or more selected sizes, scaling and positioning layers using anchors or relative centers. Resize current design requires exactly one target size. Export All Sizes saves the selected designs to a folder as PNG, JPEG or WebP, with dimensions in their file names. Existing files get unique names.

The Export room and Easy Export offer Also save thumbnail when the project has designs. Choose a design to save a JPEG next to the completed video with the same base name. Its appearance is prepared before video export starts, so later edits do not change it. An existing JPEG is kept and an error is shown rather than overwriting it.

Open Image and canvas tools

Open as New Design in the Import Image menu, or the Open Image command, creates a separate design at the image's displayed pixel size, with just that image and a transparent canvas. Dropping images on the empty Design room does the same. The import-copy preference controls embedded or linked sources. JPEG orientation is honored. Open Image supports up to 8192 pixels per side; larger images can be placed into an existing design.

Canvas offers Crop Canvas, Rotate Canvas Left/Right and Flip Canvas Horizontally/Vertically. The Crop Tool (C) is the same as Crop Canvas and works whatever layer is selected or locked. It has aspect presets and draggable handles; drag a handle past the canvas edge to make the design wider or taller, up to 8192 pixels per side, and drag inside to move the crop. Shift keeps the crop's shape and Alt scales it from the center. Enter or Apply Canvas Crop applies it; Escape or Cancel discards it. Cropping moves every layer and changes the canvas size. Image sources and masks stay intact, so they can still be repositioned. The canvas background adapts to the new size. All canvas operations can be undone.

Getting Started

Welcome to Reccut

Your illustrated, searchable guide to recording, editing, AI tools and delivery. Available offline inside Reccut.

On this pageStart with a taskFinding things fast

Reccut has four rooms, shown at the top of the window. Switch between them with Ctrl+1, Ctrl+2 and Ctrl+3.

  • Record captures your screen, camera and microphone, with cursor zoom.
  • Edit is a magnetic timeline editor with the tools and shortcuts from Final Cut Pro.
  • Export turns the project, or any file, into the format you need.
  • Design makes thumbnails and images with separate layers.

Everything belongs to a project. A project is a folder that holds the project file, your recordings, copied media, optimized media and exports.

Start with a task

  • Make your first video — a complete recording, edit and export example.
  • Connect a cloud AI Agent or use a local model.
  • Learn the editing tools and browse creative presets.
  • Solve a picture, sound or export problem.
  • Find every command or look up an unfamiliar term.

Finding things fast

This handbook opens in its own window, so you can keep it beside your project. Search covers article text, headings, examples and commands. Packaging a Project for Archiving

Copy the project and every file it uses into one folder, like Collect Files in After Effects.

Package Project (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+P) in the File menu copies the project file and all the files it uses into one new folder. The folder then holds everything needed to open the project later, on any computer, which is good for archiving and for handing a project over to someone else. Your open project is not changed.

Before packaging, you can tidy up, like Reduce Project in After Effects:

  • Remove unused media: media in the browser that is not used on the timeline is left out.
  • Remove disabled clips: clips that are switched off are removed. In the storyline they become gaps, so the edit does not move.
  • Remove switched off audio: synced audio that is switched off in every clip is left out.
  • Remove clips on hidden tracks.
  • Include optimized media: larger, but smooth to edit right away.
  • Trim media to the parts that are used: re-encode used portions of video and audio, with extra time on each side. Recording folders and still images are copied whole. Size is an estimate; re-encoding can increase it.

The window shows how many files and how much space the package needs, and warns about files that are missing. The packaging runs in the background with progress at the top of the window, and it checks that there is enough free space and that every copy is complete.

The package folder contains:

  • the project file (.reccut)
  • Footage, with every video, audio and image file
  • Recordings, with your screen and camera recordings
  • Package Report.txt, listing what was copied, what was removed and anything that was missing

Thumbnails, waveforms and proxies are not included. They are made again automatically when the package is opened. The paths in the package are relative, so the folder can be moved or renamed.

Recording Screen and Camera

Choose a screen, a camera and a microphone in the Record room. Turn on system audio to record the sound from your computer.

Under Recording → Video format, choose MP4 (H.264), MOV (H.264), MKV (H.264), or WebM (VP9 or VP8). Audio format offers codecs supported by that container when recording system audio or camera with microphone; Automatic chooses a matching codec. Microphone audio without a camera is saved as a separate audio file. With no camera and system audio off, choose M4A (AAC), Ogg (Opus), WAV (16-bit PCM), FLAC or MP3, also while recording the screen. To record only audio, choose No screen and No camera. Your choices are remembered. Formats cannot be changed during countdown, recording or saving.

Recordings are written to disk continuously for crash recovery, then finalized in the selected format when you stop. Finalizing may take a moment. PCM and FLAC use uncompressed audio capture. If finalizing fails, Reccut keeps the original recording and shows a message.

Start recording from any app

Press the record shortcut to start and stop recording: by default Control Option 0 on a Mac and Ctrl Shift 0 on Windows (the keys can be changed under Shortcuts in the Record room). The same keys with 9 pause and resume. By default these shortcuts also work from other apps while the Record room is open. They can override those combinations in other apps. Turn off Allow shortcuts in other apps under Record → Shortcuts to limit them to Reccut outside a recording. During recording, all recording shortcuts always work in the background so you can stop, pause and switch layouts. Input fields and dialogs in Reccut keep their keys. The floating recording controls and tray menu also let you pause and stop.

While you record, you can always see that the screen is being recorded, without it ending up in the video:

  • a small control bar floats over your other apps, with the time, the microphone level, Pause and Stop. It is hidden from the recording on macOS and Windows.
  • the Reccut icon in the menu bar on macOS shows a red dot and the time. On Windows the icon in the taskbar tray turns red; click it for Start, Pause, Stop and Show Reccut.
  • Hide Reccut while recording puts the Reccut window away when you start, and brings it back when the recording is saved.

Paused parts are left out of the video, and cursor zoom and layouts follow along.

Record one window

Choose a window instead of a screen under Screen. Only that window is recorded, even if it is moved or covered. Click Refresh list of windows if a new window is missing. Cursor zoom is off for window recordings.

Check the microphone

Under the microphone meter, Reccut tells you if the microphone is silent, very quiet, good or too loud. Click Test to record four seconds and hear them played back.

Input gain starts at 0 dB. Drag the slider or type a value to raise or lower the microphone level. Open Microphone mixer for input and output meters in dBFS, a peak hold, average level, a frequency spectrum, three-band EQ, an 80 Hz low cut and a compressor. Aim for speech peaks between −18 and −6 dBFS. If the input clips before gain, lower the input level on the microphone or in your system settings.

Choose Flat, Clear speech, Warm voice or Bright voice as a starting point. Changes apply to both Test and new recordings, and are saved automatically when the mixer closes and between app launches. Reset to Flat restores 0 dB gain and neutral processing. Processing is baked into the recorded microphone audio; system audio stays separate.

Use an audio interface or an external camera

Audio interfaces, USB microphones and HDMI capture devices such as Elgato Cam Link appear in the Camera and Microphone menus as soon as they are connected. If you unplug the one you chose, Reccut uses another device and goes back to yours when it returns.

Open Settings → Audio & Video for more:

  • Input channel: a microphone in input 1 of an audio interface is recorded on the left side only. Choose Left only (input 1) or Right only (input 2) to hear it on both sides. Mono mixes both inputs into one signal.
  • Output: where Reccut plays sound. System default follows your computer.
  • Resolution and Frame rate: what Reccut asks the camera for. The line below shows what it really sends. Over HDMI the format is set on the camera.
  • Delay microphone: an HDMI camera shows the picture slightly after the sound. If your lips are out of sync in the recording, increase this in small steps.

Switch between cameras

With more than one camera connected, the other cameras are listed under Camera in the Record room. Turn on the ones you want to switch to, then press Control Option C on a Mac or Ctrl Shift C on Windows to go to the next camera, before or during the recording. The cameras you turned on stay ready, so the switch happens at once.

The recording keeps one camera file that shows the camera that was active at each moment. It is made at the size of the camera that was active when you started; a camera with another shape is cropped to fill the picture. Mirror and Delay microphone apply to all cameras, so an HDMI camera and a webcam can need different values.

If Reccut or the computer crashes

The recording is written to disk while you record. If Reccut is closed by a crash or a power cut, it offers to recover the recording the next time it starts, with layouts and cursor movements up to the last few seconds.

Optimized Media

Convert clips to a high quality format that is smooth to edit.

Camera files are often hard to decode, which makes skimming and playback heavy. Optimize Media (Ctrl+Alt+O) converts the selected clips in the background:

  • 8-bit clips become H.264 with a keyframe every half second, at very high quality.
  • 10-bit and HDR clips become 10-bit VP9, so the extra precision is kept for color correction.

The files are saved in the Optimized Media folder of the project and used for playback from then on. Progress is shown at the top of the window, where you can also cancel it. The original files are never changed.

You can also choose Create optimized media when you import.

Proxies and export. Formats the preview cannot play directly, such as ProRes, DNxHD, MPEG-2, interlaced and HDR video, get a proxy when you import them: a copy of at most 1920 pixels that is light to edit. H.264, HEVC, VP9 and AV1 play directly. By default the export reads the original files, also ProRes, so the proxy never limits the quality. The proxy and the export prepare the picture the same way (HDR is tone mapped, other color spaces become Rec. 709), so color corrections look the same in the export as in the preview. Choose Source media › Optimized media in the export settings for a faster export from the copies you edit with; files that only have a proxy are then at most 1920 pixels. If an original is missing, the export uses the proxy or optimized media.

HDR, 10-bit and RAW

How Reccut handles HDR video, 10-bit clips and camera RAW photos.

HDR video (PQ and HLG, for example from iPhones and mirrorless cameras) is detected when you import it, and marked HDR in the browser. Reccut shows and exports it tone mapped to normal SDR, so it looks right on every screen, and keeps 10 bits in the working copy for color correction. Exporting in HDR is not supported yet.

10-bit clips keep their precision: optimized media stays 10-bit, and color correction is calculated in 16-bit floating point.

Camera RAW photos (CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, DNG, RAF, ORF, RW2 and more) are imported using the full size JPEG the camera stores inside the file, with the camera's white balance and the right orientation. The RAW data itself is not developed.

RAW video is decoded with the camera maker's own software. When it is installed, Reccut converts the clip to ProRes 4444 when you import it, next to the original file, and uses that file from then on: - Blackmagic RAW: Blackmagic RAW from Blackmagic Design (or DaVinci Resolve). - RED R3D and Nikon N-RAW: REDCINE-X PRO from RED. - ARRIRAW: ARRI Reference Tool (command line) from ARRI. The clip becomes Rec. 709. - ProRes RAW on a Mac: Apple's own decoder (Pro Video Formats, or Final Cut Pro).

If the software is missing, Reccut shows a link to the maker's page, and Locate if it is installed somewhere else. Canon Cinema RAW Light has no decoder other apps can use, so export those clips to ProRes in Canon's Cinema RAW Development first.

LOG footage (Apple Log, S-Log3, V-Log, F-Log, LogC3) looks flat until it is converted to normal colors. Choose the camera in Camera LUT in the Info tab, or choose a .cube LUT file from the camera maker. It applies to every clip from the file, in the viewer and the export, before the color correction.

The Info tab in the inspector shows the bit depth, HDR format and which file is used for playback.

With Add to the timeline, they are stacked above the storyline, the bottom layer lowest, and grouped so they move together. Animate each layer in the Video tab, for example to slide a logo in.

Synchronizing Audio

Line up sound from an external recorder with your video automatically.

On this pageIn the browser: merge before you editOn the timeline: place audio under the video

Reccut compares the sound in your video clips with audio files from an external recorder or microphone, and finds where they match. It works on the loudness of the sound, so different microphones and levels do not matter.

In the browser: merge before you edit

Select the video clips and the audio files that belong to them (for example everything with Ctrl+A), then choose Synchronize Audio (Ctrl+Alt+G) or right click and choose it there.

  • Each audio file is added to the matching video clips as an extra audio track. One long recording can match many video clips, and one clip can get audio from several recorders.
  • The camera audio is switched off, but kept. When you drag the clip to the timeline it brings both tracks, with the camera audio switched off. Switch between them whenever you like.
  • Remove Synchronized Audio (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G) takes the merged audio away again.

On the timeline: place audio under the video

Put an audio clip below the timeline, select it and choose Auto Sync Audio (Ctrl+Alt+G), or right click it.

  • If the audio is longer than the video, it is trimmed to the video. If it is shorter, it is just moved to the right place.
  • Every video clip is compared with the whole recording, so each piece is placed where its sound matches, even when the clips are in a different order than they were recorded.
  • If the audio matches several clips, you choose Sync to First Clip or Split and Place, which puts a piece under each clip.
  • The synced audio can be grouped with the video, and the camera audio switched off (but kept). These choices are remembered, and are also in Preferences.
  • Select several audio clips to sync them all at once, or select video clips too to sync only to those.

Tools

Select, Trim, Position, Range, Blade, Zoom and Hand.

On this pageSelect: ordinary editingTrim: roll, slip and slidePosition: preserve the empty timeRange: work on a time intervalBlade: split before removingZoom and Hand: navigate a long edit

The tools are in the toolbar above the timeline. The pointer changes to show what will happen.

  • Select (A) selects, moves and trims clips.
  • Trim (T) rolls the edit between two clips, slips the content inside a clip, or slides a clip with Option (Alt).
  • Position (P) moves clips freely and leaves gaps behind.
  • Range (R) selects a part of the timeline.
  • Blade (B) cuts where you click.
  • Zoom (Z) zooms in where you click; Option (Alt) click zooms out.
  • Hand (H) drags the timeline around.

Select: ordinary editing

Press A, click a clip, and drag it to reorder it or change its lane. Drag an edge to trim. Example: move the second demonstration shot before the third, then shorten its ending. The magnetic storyline closes up around the edit.

Trim: roll, slip and slide

Press T. Drag the shared edge between two adjacent clips on the same track to roll it: one clip grows while the other shrinks, keeping their combined duration and outer edges. This works on every video and audio track, including voice-over and the captions track. The clips must touch; an edge next to a gap trims only that clip. Example: move a cut later because the first shot ends too soon, or move the boundary between two captions without changing when the pair starts and ends.

Drag inside a clip to slip its source content while its timeline position and duration stay fixed. Over the middle of a video or audio clip, the pointer shows two brackets back to back with arrows pointing out. Images, titles and captions have no source to slip. Example: show a later part of a screen recording without changing the surrounding narration. Hold Option/Alt while dragging for a slide, which moves the clip and adjusts its neighbours. Available source material limits these edits.

Position: preserve the empty time

Press P when a clip needs a fixed timeline position and gaps are intentional. Example: move a shot later to leave room for a title card. Unlike the magnetic Select move, this can leave a gap and overwrite content at the destination; inspect the result and use Undo if needed.

Range: work on a time interval

Press R and drag over the timeline to mark an interval. The marked interval can define an export range or the duration of a new effect/redaction. Example: mark just the five seconds containing a private email address, then add a redaction. I and O also set range start and end.

Blade: split before removing

Press B and click the point where you want two clips instead of one. Then press A, select the unwanted piece, and delete it. Example: cut on both sides of a failed sentence and remove only that piece. The keyboard Blade command cuts at the playhead instead of the mouse position.

Zoom and Hand: navigate a long edit

Press Z and click around a cut to inspect it more closely; Option/Alt-click to zoom out. Press H and drag to pan across the timeline without moving clips. Use Zoom to Fit (Shift+Z) to see the whole edit, then return to A for clip selection.

Set Photo duration and choose browser order, file-name order or a shuffled order. The slideshow appends after existing timeline content. Photo duration is the spacing between cuts; transitions are centered on those cuts, so they do not shorten the total slideshow.

Transitions

Choose None for straight cuts, Cross Dissolve only, or Random from selected types. With Random, check the types you want. Choices are made once when the slideshow is created and saved with the clips; they do not change during playback. Transition length is limited to less than half a photo so neighboring transitions do not overlap. You can edit each transition later on the timeline.

Framing and motion

  • Fit entire image keeps the complete photo visible.
  • Fill frame crops enough to cover the sequence.
  • Fill height and Fill width preserve proportions and match that dimension; the other dimension can be cropped or leave empty space.
  • Stretch fills both dimensions and changes proportions.

Choose None, Ken Burns · pan & zoom, or Slow pan for movement. Motion amount controls the zoom. Directions vary across images, and movement eases at the endpoints. An image’s motion remains continuous when the clip is split.

For a single image, apply Ken Burns from Effects → Photo motion, or enable it in Video → Image framing & motion. Choose zoom in/out or a pan direction and adjust Zoom / travel. Image fit, cropping and ordinary Transform controls remain editable.

The foreground stays clear. Adjust Background blur and Darkening in the dialog, or edit the background clip’s effects and color afterward.

The image and its background share timing and transition as a single picture, including portrait-to-landscape changes. Clicking either clip lets you edit its appearance independently; timing edits, splits, copies and deletion keep the pair together.

Multicam Clips

Create and synchronize camera angles, choose the program live or one section at a time, adjust sound, recover drafts and continue editing on the timeline.

On this pageQuick startMake a multicam clipThe multicam windowSynchronize the camerasSwitch camerasThe multicam timelineSoundAdd it to the timelineChange it laterSave and recover an unfinished editKeyboard referenceTroubleshootingTips

A multicam clip holds the clips from several cameras that recorded the same thing at the same time, for example an interview filmed from the front and from the side. Reccut lines them up by their sound. You watch all the cameras at once and press a number to choose which one is shown. Reccut cuts the clips for you, so only one camera shows at a time, and the sound comes from the best microphone the whole way.

Quick start

  1. Import the video from each camera into the project. Add a separate audio recording too, if you have one.
  2. Select the files in the media browser and choose File › New Multicam Clip…. Give the clip a name at the top left.
  3. If the files recorded the same event with shared sound, press Auto Sync. Check the result and line up any remaining files by hand.
  4. Put the playhead where the edit should start and choose a camera in Angles, or press its number. Program begins at that position.
  5. Press Space and use 1–9 to switch cameras as you watch. Press Space again when you reach the end you want.
  6. Choose the sound source and make any level adjustments. Review the Program track, then press Add to Timeline at the top right.

The city footage in these pictures demonstrates camera choices and the editing controls. These are different city shots rather than cameras recording one event, and their audio tracks contain silence. The separate audio example is extracted from camera 4. Use recordings with shared, audible sound when following the audio synchronization steps with your own footage.

Make a multicam clip

There are two ways to start:

  • From clips on the timeline. Put the clips from each camera on top of each other, select them and right click one of them. Choose Create Multicam Clip…. Their positions are kept. Program starts empty so you can choose the cameras yourself.
  • From files. Choose File › New Multicam Clip…, or right click an empty part of the timeline. Files you selected in the browser are added straight away. You add more in the window.

Starting from timeline clips keeps the originals in place while the multicam window is open. Add to Timeline replaces those selected clips when you apply the result.

Angles accept video files and separate audio files. Still images and Reccut screen-and-camera recordings cannot be used as angles. An already-added file has a check mark in Files. Use Ctrl click on Windows or Cmd click on Mac to select several files, and Shift click to select a range, then use Add Files. Double clicking adds one file immediately.

The multicam window

The window has five parts:

  1. Files on the left. All video and audio in the project. Double click a file, or drag it to Angles or the timeline, to add it. Switch between list, icon and filmstrip view at the top. The + button imports new files into the project.
  2. Angles. All cameras at once, in small views numbered 1, 2, 3 in the order they were added. The views always form an even grid, so three cameras get four views and the empty one is black with the Reccut R. No video is stretched.
  3. Program. What goes on the timeline. A green frame marks the active camera in Angles. The camera numbers keep their own colors.
  4. Properties on the right. Which camera to use when a camera has no picture, and the sound settings.
  5. The multicam timeline at the bottom, with the sound, the program and one track for each camera.

The blue Add to Timeline button at the top right puts the result on the timeline.

The camera tracks contain the available source footage. Program contains the choices you have made. A new multicam clip starts with a blank Program even though its camera tracks already show video. Playing or skimming before the first camera choice keeps Program empty.

Make room for the pictures

The two buttons at the top right of the window hide and show Files and Properties, the same buttons as in Edit. With both hidden, Angles and Program get the whole width. Drag the dividers between the parts to change their size, and the divider above the timeline to change its height. The window remembers the layout.

Synchronize the cameras

Press Auto Sync at the top. Reccut compares the sound in every file and lines them up, so the same moment is at the same place on the timeline. Everything with sound can be synchronized, also a separate audio recorder.

If a file cannot be synchronized, a message at the top says which one and why. For example, the file has no sound, the sound is still being analyzed, or no matching sound was found because it was recorded at another time. Files that match are synchronized even when others fail. A silent audio track also has no useful sound to compare. Wait for background analysis to finish before trying again.

To line up one file by hand, drag its clip sideways in its track. Snapping (N) helps you hit the start or end of the other clips. To synchronize only one file, for example one you just added, right click its clip and choose Synchronize to Audio. The other files stay where they are.

After synchronization, check a shared event such as a clap, a word or an object touching a surface. Review the beginning and end of a long recording. Synchronization adjusts where files start, so visible timing differences later in a recording still need to be reviewed.

Switch cameras

New multicam clips start with an empty Program. Choose a camera to begin, then switch cameras manually or during playback. Only the sections you have reviewed and chosen are added to the result; unreviewed gaps and the remaining tail stay empty. Opening an existing multicam clip keeps its previous choices and lets you continue beyond its old endpoint.

  • Press 1 to 9 to cut to that camera at the playhead. This works while the video plays, so you can switch cameras live, like a director.
  • When paused, the choice applies from the playhead to the next existing cut or the end of the chosen section. A first choice in an empty section starts with one frame. You can try different cameras at the same position without changing the choices before or after that section.
  • Click a camera in Angles to do the same.
  • Hold Shift with the number, or Shift click, to change the camera of the piece you are in without making a new cut.
  • Each cut shows in the Program track with the number and color of the camera.

The number keys address the first nine video angles, including the numeric keypad. Click an angle to choose a camera beyond number 9. Separate audio sources use A1, A2 and do not take a video shortcut.

Camera choices always use the playhead. Skimming previews the frame under the pointer. Move the playhead to that position before choosing a camera there.

Choose sections while paused

  1. Put the playhead at the first frame you want and press 1, or click camera 1. The first choice in an empty part of Program covers one frame.
  2. Move forward to the next change and choose camera 2. Camera 1 fills the interval between the previous choice and this change.
  3. Keep moving forward and choosing cameras. At the last frame you want to include, choose the current camera again to extend the reviewed section through that frame.
  4. To correct an existing section, move the playhead inside it and choose another camera. That changes the picture from the playhead to its next cut or existing end. Earlier and later sections keep their choices.
  5. To replace the whole current section, hold Shift while choosing the camera. The existing section boundaries are kept.

Moving the playhead through an empty part alone does not add video. A later choice can leave an unreviewed gap between separate sections. Review the Program track before applying the edit.

Switch during playback

Choose the first camera and press Space. The chosen camera extends as playback passes through new footage. Press another number at each change. Pause to inspect or correct a section, then resume to continue. Playback can also extend a choice while reviewing backward with J. Existing later cuts still bound the sections being extended.

Older saved multicam clips can already have camera choices across their full length. Opening one keeps those saved choices. New multicam clips use the empty-Program workflow described here.

When a camera has no picture

Cameras often start and stop at different times. If the camera you cut to has no picture at that moment, Reccut shows another camera instead, so the video never goes black. Those pieces are striped in the Program track. Under Properties, choose which camera to use instead in When a camera has no picture:

  • Use the first camera with a picture (the default).
  • Use camera 2, or any other camera, as the fallback. If that camera has no picture either, the first camera with a picture is used.
  • Leave a gap, for an empty space instead.

You can also right click a camera's clip and choose Use as Fallback Camera.

Change the angle by hand

Right click anywhere in the multicam timeline and choose Active Video Angle to show another camera in the piece you clicked, without making a new cut. Active Audio Angle chooses where the sound comes from: one of the cameras, a separate audio file or Follow the Picture. The current choice has a check mark.

The same menus are on the multicam clip in the Edit timeline. Right click a clip from the multicam clip and choose Active Video Angle or Active Audio Angle. The clip changes camera, or the sound changes, without opening the multicam window. The clip keeps its trim, effects, keyframes and transitions; only the camera changes. If the camera has no picture for the whole clip, Reccut says so and leaves the clip as it is.

The multicam timeline

From the top:

  • Sound: the sound that is used, with its level.
  • Program: the cuts, which go on the timeline.
  • One track per camera, named 1, 2, 3 with the keyboard shortcut next to the name. The headphones use the sound from that file, and the sliders button opens its settings.
  • New camera: an empty track. Drag a video here from Files to add another camera.
  • Separate sound: audio recorded separately, named A1, A2.

Sound, Program and the ruler stay at the top when you scroll down through many cameras.

Track height

Program is a little taller than the other tracks. To make any track taller or lower, drag the bottom edge of its header on the left. Hold Shift while you drag a camera or sound track to change all of them at once. Double click the edge to go back to the standard height.

The timeline works like the one in Edit:

  • Zoom with Cmd + and Cmd - (Ctrl on Windows), pinch, or the slider. Shift Z or Fit shows everything.
  • Scroll sideways with the trackpad, Shift and the mouse wheel, or the Hand tool (H).
  • Skimming (S) shows the frame under the pointer in Angles and Program. Snapping (N) makes cuts and clips snap to each other and to the playhead.
  • Select (A) and Trim (T): drag a cut sideways to fine tune it. Blade (B): click the Program track to add a cut.
  • Backspace or Delete removes the selected cut, or the one under the playhead. If the previous section touches it, the previous camera continues through that section. Removing the first or an isolated choice can leave Program empty there.
  • Up and Down Arrow jump between cuts. Left and Right Arrow step one frame.
  • I and O set which part goes on the timeline. The rest is darker. Alt X clears it.
  • Space plays and pauses, and J, K and L play backward, pause and forward.
  • The window has its own Undo (Ctrl+Z) and Redo (Ctrl+Shift+Z).

Dragging a cut changes the boundary between adjacent choices. It stops at the neighboring cuts and at the existing end of the reviewed section. Zoom in for frame-level adjustments. Removing a cut or a camera in this window changes the multicam definition. Source files remain available in the project browser.

Choose an output range

Put the playhead at the desired start and press I, then move to the desired end and press O. The region outside the range is shaded. Alt X on Windows or Option X on Mac clears the range.

I/O marks limit which part of the chosen Program is sent to the timeline. They do not choose cameras in empty areas. Without explicit I/O marks, the output starts at the first chosen section and ends at the last chosen section. Empty space before and after those sections is excluded. Gaps between chosen sections keep their timing in the output. A fixed audio source can continue through these internal video gaps.

Change the order of the tracks

Drag a track by its header on the left, up or down. The numbers and the keyboard shortcuts follow the new order, so the camera you drag to the top becomes camera 1. Separate sound tracks can be reordered among themselves in the same way.

Add a camera afterwards

You can add cameras at any time, also to a multicam clip that is already on the timeline. Open it with Edit Multicam Clip, then drag a video from Files to the New camera track, or to the place in time where it belongs. Then right click it and choose Synchronize to Audio.

You can also double click a file in Files to add it. Right click a camera track and choose Remove from Multicam Clip, or use the X in its header, to remove an angle. Review Program afterwards because choices that used the removed angle need another available camera. Undo restores the previous setup.

Remove source media from the project

In the main media browser, select a file and press Backspace or Delete to open its removal dialog. The dialog lists affected multicam clips and any timeline use. Removing a source also removes multicam clips that depend on it, across the project's sequences.

Remove from Project keeps the files on disk and supports Undo. The red Delete from Disk button also moves the source files to the Windows Recycle Bin or Mac Trash. Undo restores project entries, so restore trashed files too before continuing to use them. Don’t show this again applies to project-removal warnings. Disk deletion still uses the dialog.

Sound

Sound recorded separately

Sound from an external recorder or a lavalier microphone is usually better than the sound in the cameras. Add the audio file like a camera: double click it in Files, or drag it to the Separate sound part of the timeline. It is named A1 and is used for the sound straight away. Press Auto Sync, or right click it and choose Synchronize to Audio, to line it up.

Choose where the sound comes from

Choose the sound at the top of the window, in Properties, with the headphones in a track, or by right clicking a clip and choosing Use Sound from This File:

  • One of the cameras.
  • One of the separate audio files.
  • Sound follows the picture: the sound switches together with the camera.

The Sound track

The sound that is used lies at the top of the timeline, so it is easy to see and adjust:

  • Drag the yellow line up or down to change the level. The level shows on the left.
  • Alt (Option) click the line to add a keyframe. Drag a keyframe up or down to change the level there, or sideways to move it.
  • Drag the line between two keyframes to move both.
  • Right click a keyframe to delete it, or right click the track to remove all keyframes.

Adjust the sound

Under Properties, choose the file in Adjust and set:

  • Level: the volume. With keyframes, Level moves the whole line up or down.
  • Channels: Both channels, Left channel only, Right channel only, Swap left and right or Mix to mono. Use one side when a microphone was only recorded on the left or right channel, which is common with external recorders and cameras with two microphone inputs.
  • Gain: turns the sound up or down before the other settings.
  • EQ: a starting point such as Voice Boost or Podcast, and Bass, Mid and Treble to adjust it.
  • Remove rumble: removes low noise from wind, traffic and handling.
  • Reduce noise: removes steady hiss and fan noise.
  • AI Clean Voice: removes background noise such as fans, traffic, hum and room sound from speech, with a Strength slider. The first time, Reccut cleans the sound in the background; you hear the original until it is done. It is the same AI Clean Voice as in the Audio tab in Edit, it runs on this computer and nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Reset Sound sets everything back.

Nothing is changed automatically. You hear the settings while you play in the multicam window, and they follow the sound to the timeline as the clip level, keyframes, AI Clean Voice and ordinary audio effects. You can fine tune them later in the Audio tab in Edit.

Add it to the timeline

Press the blue Add to Timeline button at the top right, or Cmd Return (Ctrl Enter on Windows). The result is a group of clips with an MC badge: one video clip for each cut, and the sound below.

  • Made from clips on the timeline, the multicam clip replaces them in the same place.
  • Made from files, it is added at the end of the storyline.

The first selected source frame becomes the start of the new timeline result. The output ends at the last selected frame within the I/O range, even when the available camera files are much longer. Sound is cropped to that output span. With Sound follows the picture, the selected camera supplies the sound for its section.

Program needs at least one camera choice before it can be added. If the window says to choose a camera, return to the intended start frame and select an angle.

Change it later

  • Double click the multicam clip to edit the clips inside like any other clips, for example to add effects, color or titles. Press Esc when you are done.
  • Cmd double click (Ctrl double click on Windows), right click and choose Edit Multicam Clip, or press Edit Multicam Clip in the inspector, to open the multicam window again. Switch cameras, add cameras or change the sound, then press Update Timeline.

Work you did on the timeline is kept. When the window opens, it reads the clips as they are: cuts you moved or trimmed, clips you split with the blade, a trimmed start or end, and the level and keyframes of the sound. When you update, every piece gets back its effects, keyframes, color, transitions and on or off state, and a trimmed audio clip keeps its length.

To continue after the old end, move the playhead to the next position you want, choose a camera and keep reviewing. New picture and automatically generated sound can extend past the old output endpoint. An I/O range you set yourself still limits the output until you change or clear it. Audio trims made deliberately on the Edit timeline are kept.

There is one exception. If you deleted a piece in the middle of the multicam clip, or changed the speed of one of its clips, that cannot be described as cuts. The window then tells you, and asks before Update Timeline puts the removed part back. Switching a camera with Active Video Angle on the timeline always keeps everything.

Save and recover an unfinished edit

Changes are saved automatically as a local draft while you work. The header changes from Saving draft… to Draft saved locally. Saving a draft also happens when you close with Esc or the X. The Edit timeline receives the result when you use Add to Timeline or Update Timeline.

  1. Open the same project and sequence.
  2. Choose File › Resume Multicam Draft…, or use the recovery notification. If several drafts are available, select the one you want.
  3. Continue from the restored camera choices and playhead position.
  4. Apply the result to the timeline when it is ready. This clears the completed draft.

Drafts belong to each project and sequence and stay in this computer's Reccut profile. They are not included in the project file or transferred with it. For a project you plan to move or share, apply the multicam result and save the project first. An unexpected shutdown can lose changes since the most recent draft save.

Use the trash button beside Add to Timeline or Update Timeline to discard the current draft. Reccut asks before removing those unfinished changes.

If the timeline changed after a draft was started, Reccut warns before applying it replaces the current multicam clips.

If the header says Draft could not be saved, keep the window open and check available storage. Applying a finished result to the timeline and saving the project also preserves that edit.

Keyboard reference

These keys apply while the multicam window is active and you are not typing in a field. On Mac, use Cmd for the Ctrl combinations below and Option for Alt.

  • 1–9 or the numeric keypad choose a camera at the playhead. Shift 1–9 switches the current section without adding a cut.
  • Space plays or pauses. J plays backward, K pauses and L plays forward. Repeated J or L increases the speed up to 8×.
  • Left/Right Arrow steps one frame. Shift Left/Right Arrow moves one second. Up/Down Arrow jumps between cuts. Home/End goes to the current range boundaries.
  • I/O marks the output range. Alt X clears it.

  • S toggles skimming. N toggles snapping.

  • Backspace/Delete removes a selected cut or the cut under the playhead.
  • Ctrl Z undoes within multicam. Ctrl Shift Z or Ctrl Y redoes.
  • Ctrl Enter adds or updates the timeline. Esc closes and saves the draft.

Troubleshooting

Program is black or Add to Timeline asks for a camera

A new Program is empty until you choose an angle. A gap after the last reviewed section is also empty. Place the playhead on a chosen section to review it, or choose a camera where the new section should start. If the selected source itself has ended, check the fallback setting in Properties.

The camera choice happens somewhere other than the preview

With skimming on, the pointer can preview another time while the playhead stays in place. Camera shortcuts use the playhead. Click the ruler to move it, or toggle skimming off with S, then choose the camera.

Auto Sync cannot find a match

Read the reason for each file at the top. Finish media analysis first. Confirm that both files contain audible recordings of the same event. Silent tracks, unrelated clips and files without sound need manual alignment. After adding a new angle, Synchronize to Audio adjusts that file alone.

There is no sound, or it comes from the wrong camera

Choose Sound comes from at the top or in Properties. Check that the source actually contains audible audio and overlaps the output interval. In Sound follows the picture, choosing a silent camera also chooses its silence. Use one continuous microphone source when the sound should stay consistent. Check Level, Gain and Channels, and try Reset Sound if needed.

A different camera appears after a source ends

Check When a camera has no picture in Properties. The default uses another camera with available picture and marks that Program section with stripes. Choose a specific fallback camera, choose a camera that covers the interval, or use Leave a gap.

New choices are missing after Update Timeline

Check the I/O marks. Clear a deliberate range with Alt X or Option X, or set a later Out point. Confirm that the new section appears in Program and that you used Update Timeline after making the choices.

A draft seems to be missing

Return to the project and sequence where it was started, using the same Reccut profile on the same computer. Open File › Resume Multicam Draft…. Applying or explicitly discarding a draft removes it from this list. Once applied, reopen it through Edit Multicam Clip on the timeline.

Tips

  • Start every camera before the action begins and clap once in front of all of them. A clear sound makes Auto Sync more reliable.
  • Keep the camera sound on even when you use an external recorder. Auto Sync needs it to find the right place.
  • Reccut recordings already switch between screen and camera, so they cannot be used as cameras in a multicam clip.

Stabilization

Smooth out shaky handheld video, like in Final Cut Pro.

Select a video clip and choose Stabilize Clip (Alt+Shift+S), or turn on Stabilize in the Video tab. Reccut analyzes the camera motion in the background (progress is shown at the top), and the clip is stabilized as soon as the analysis is done. The analysis is saved, so it only runs once per file. To have it ready before you edit, turn on Analyze video for stabilization in Settings > Import.

  • Automatic chooses for you: if the camera hardly moves it is locked like on a tripod, otherwise the movement is smoothed.
  • Smooth keeps the camera movement, but takes out the shake. Smooth X, Smooth Y and Smooth rotation set how much each direction is smoothed. At 0 a direction is left as it is.
  • Tripod removes all camera movement in the clip.
  • Zoom: the picture is zoomed in just enough to hide the edges that moving it would show. Automatic shows how much. Choose Manual to set the zoom yourself; with less zoom, edges can show when the correction is large.

The same correction is used in the viewer and in exports. How it works: Reccut follows hundreds of points from frame to frame, finds the camera movement (sideways, up and down, and rotation) while ignoring things that move in the picture, smooths the path and moves each frame from the shaky path to the smooth one.

Tracking: Attach Clips to Moving Objects

Follow a face, a sign or a product in the video, and pin a redaction, title or image to it.

On this page1. Track the object2. Attach a clipRedactions in one stepWith the AI Agent

Tracking follows something that moves in a video or screen recording, and lets other clips follow it. Use it to keep a redaction on a moving face or license plate, or to pin a title, an arrow or an image to an object. It works in two steps, so you can track first and decide later what should follow.

1. Track the object

  1. Select the video clip and move the playhead to where the object is clearly visible.
  2. In the Video tab, under Tracking, turn on Show area. Drag the dashed box over the object and resize it so it fits tightly.
  3. Press Track Forward. Reccut follows the object from the playhead to the end of the clip (progress is shown on the button and at the top).

The result is saved on the clip as Track 1, Track 2 and so on, with its length. Delete a track with the trash button. If the object disappears for more than half a second, tracking stops there and tells you when.

Choose an area with detail, such as a face, text or a logo. Plain surfaces, motion blur and things that are covered are hard to follow. If tracking stops early, move the playhead to where it stopped and track again from there.

2. Attach a clip

  1. Put the clip that should follow on a track above the video, for example a redaction, a title or an image, and place it where it should be relative to the object.
  2. Select it. Under Follow Track choose the track and the mode, and press Attach.

The clip keeps the place it has at the playhead relative to the object, also when it is beside it. It is grouped with the video, so they move together on the timeline. Double click a clip in the group to select only that clip.

  • Live (default): the clip always follows the track as it is now. Track again, or move, scale or stabilize the video, and the clip follows without attaching it again. Drag the clip in the viewer to change where it sits relative to the object.
  • Keyframes: writes position, rotation and size keyframes you can adjust one by one. Tracking keyframes: Remove deletes only the keyframes tracking wrote; your own keyframes outside the tracked part stay.
  • Rotation makes the clip turn with the object.
  • Scale (experimental) makes the clip grow and shrink as the object comes closer or moves away. Real perspective, like a sign seen from the side, is not followed.

Detach stops a live link. The clip stays grouped with the video; ungroup it with Ctrl+Shift+G if you want.

Redactions in one step

For a redaction, put it over what it should hide and press Track Forward under Follow Track. Reccut tracks what the redaction covers in the video below and attaches the redaction to it.

With the AI Agent

Ask the agent, for example "Blur the face of the woman in the pink shirt" or "Put a label that follows the car". It finds the object in the frames, adds the redaction or title, tracks the object and attaches the clip. You can adjust the result as usual.

How it works: Reccut finds corners inside the area, follows them from frame to frame and works out how the area moves, turns and changes size, ignoring points that do not move with the rest. Tracking and following work the same in the viewer and in exports.

Masks

Show only part of a clip, with shapes you can animate.

On this pageDraw with the penSettingsAnimate a mask

A mask decides which part of a clip is visible. On a Blur, Pixelate or other effect clip, the mask decides where the effect works. Use it to cut out a person, frame a detail, make a spotlight, blend two clips, or hide part of the picture.

  • Select a video, image, recording or title clip and choose Add Rectangle Mask (Alt+M), Add Ellipse Mask (Alt+Shift+M) or Add Freeform Mask in the Edit menu, or use the buttons in the Masks tab of the inspector.
  • Click Edit in Viewer to show the shape in the viewer. Drag it to move it, and drag the handles to resize it. Hold Shift to keep the shape, and Option (Alt) to resize from the center.
  • Freeform masks have points: drag a point to move it, double click the outline to add a point, and Option (Alt) click a point to remove it. Turn on Smooth curve for soft curves through the points.

Draw with the pen

Choose Draw with Pen in the Masks tab, or Draw Mask with Pen (Ctrl+Alt+M), like the pen tool in Premiere Pro. Click in the viewer to place points. Drag a little while you click to make the shape smooth. Click the first point, double click or press Return to close the shape. Backspace removes the last point and Escape cancels. The shape becomes a freeform mask that you can adjust and animate.

Settings

  • Mode: Add shows what is inside the shape, Subtract cuts the shape out, and Intersect keeps only where it overlaps the masks above it. Several masks can be combined.
  • Invert turns the mask inside out.
  • Feather gives the mask a soft edge, from slightly soft to a wide fade. Expansion makes the mask bigger or smaller around its edge, without moving the points. Opacity lets some of the hidden part show through.

Animate a mask

Position, size, rotation, feather and opacity have their own keyframe buttons. For a freeform mask, the keyframe button next to Shape saves all the points at the playhead. Move the playhead, drag the points to the new shape, and Reccut moves every point smoothly between the keyframes, like rotoscoping. Points you add or remove are added or removed in every keyframe, so the animation keeps working.

Use a redaction to hide something in the picture, such as a password, an email address, a face or a license plate. A redaction is a clip of its own above the video, and it affects everything below it.

  • Choose Add Pixelate (Alt+P) or Add Blur (Alt+Shift+P) in the Edit menu, or drag Pixelate, Blur, Solid or Frosted from the Redact section of the Effects browser (Ctrl+5) to the timeline.
  • It is placed above everything at the playhead. If a range is marked (I and O), it covers the range; otherwise it lasts to the end of the clip below.

Effects for the whole frame

For full manual control, use Blur, Pixelate or Frosted Glass from the Whole Frame section of the Effects browser, or Add Blur Effect and Add Pixelate Effect in the Edit menu. The effect covers the whole picture, like an adjustment layer in Premiere Pro. Then draw a mask with the pen (Ctrl+Alt+M) in the Masks tab to choose where it works. Several masks can be combined, each with feather, expansion and keyframes, so the shape can follow and change with what you hide. You can also set the shape of any redaction to Whole Frame.

Styles

  • Pixelate turns the area into large blocks. Good for text and faces. With Whole blocks at edge (on for new redactions) the blocks along the edge are kept whole instead of being cut by the shape, so the edge follows the blocks and can reach a little outside the shape. Turn it off for an exact edge.
  • Blur makes it soft. Use a high amount for text.
  • Solid covers the area with a color. This is the safest for passwords, because nothing of the original is left.
  • Frosted combines blur and blocks, like frosted glass.

In the Redact tab of the inspector you can choose the shape (rectangle, rounded, ellipse or Freeform), the amount, a soft edge, rotation and Redact outside, which hides everything except the area.

Don’t show this again disables the warning for future removals; restore it in Settings → General → Projects → Warn before removing adjusted effects. Removing an effect and all its settings or keyframes is one Undo step.

Drop Shadow follows the visible pixels after keying, cropping and masks. Amount reaches 500% for large shadows. Choose a Color, adjust Softness (0% gives a sharp edge), set Opacity, and move the shadow with Position X/Y in project pixels. Amount, softness, opacity and position support keyframes. Adjustment layers

Choose Adjustment layer at the top of the Effects browser, or Edit → Add Adjustment Layer. This creates an empty clip on a video track above the picture. It covers the marked In/Out range when one is set, otherwise the remainder of the storyline clip at the playhead (or five seconds in empty space). Trim, extend, move, split or duplicate it like a clip.

The layer processes the combined picture on every video track below it, only during its duration. Video clips and titles on higher tracks, and captions, are unaffected. Add effects in Effects, set exposure, white balance, wheels or Film Looks in Color, and draw Masks to limit the affected region. Video → Layer strength blends the whole look, and Fade in/out can ease it in. A blank layer leaves the picture unchanged. Switch the layer off to compare. Multiple adjustment layers process in track order.

Stylize effects

The Stylize section includes Digital noise, Film grain, Digital glitch, VHS / analog tape, RGB split, Chromatic aberration, Scanlines, and Glow / bloom. Click to apply to selected video/image/recording clips or an adjustment layer. With no eligible selection, a click creates an adjustment layer carrying the effect. Drag onto a clip to apply it there, or onto an empty video track for a new adjustment layer. The same effects are available under Add stack effect….

Noise, film grain, glitch and VHS animate from the timeline, so seeking and exporting a frame reproduce the same pattern. Speed 0 freezes animation and Seed changes its random pattern. Parameters in the stack are static; layer strength, color controls and masks support keyframes.

VHS treats the whole picture as a soft, low-bandwidth tape image. Color bleeds more than luminance, static changes from frame to frame, and brief tracking faults occasionally bend the picture. Choose an SP, LP or EP-inspired preset, then adjust Picture softness, Color bleed, Static noise and Tracking faults. Advanced includes chroma delay, subtle scanlines, faded color, speed and seed. These are creative tape simulations, not a measured hardware-deck profile.

Film grain uses irregular, spatially correlated texture and a tonal response instead of digital static. Start with fine 35mm-inspired grain, use the 16mm preset for stronger texture, or aged 8mm for optional dust, scratches, gate weave and flicker. These are procedural film-inspired looks, not scanned stock samples. Grain size is consistent across output sizes.

RGB split offsets channels uniformly in a chosen direction. Chromatic aberration models radial lens fringing: it leaves the lens center aligned and increases separation toward the edges. Use a low Amount for subtle optics, and Advanced to move the lens center.

Start with light film grain and glow for a subtle look. Use RGB split and glitch in short bursts around edits. Keep important text on a track above the adjustment layer for legibility.

Solid color and gradient ramp

Choose Solid color or Gradient ramp under Color fills in the Effects browser, or from Add stack effect… in the inspector. These effects fill a video, image or recording source. Strength blends the fill with the source. Turn on Preserve source alpha to keep transparent areas, for example after a color key.

  • Linear: drag the start and end handles to set the direction and length, or enter Angle in degrees and choose a preset. 0° runs left to right and 90° runs down. Click the color bar once to add a color. Click a round color handle to select it, then change its color or position below.
  • Radial: drag the center and radius handles. Color stops run from the center to the outer radius. Center and Radius also have numeric controls.
  • Freeform: double-click the preview to add a color point. Drag points to place them, choose their colors, and adjust Point spread with the square handle or numeric control. Larger spread gives a color more influence. Freeform uses smooth point blending; it does not include Illustrator's gradient lines.

Add point/stop and Remove point/stop also work. A gradient supports up to 32 stops or points. Arrow keys move focused handles; Shift makes larger steps. Edits support undo and redo and are saved in the project. Gradient settings are static. Preview and export use the same renderer. Design fills use these same controls.

Keying and Background Removal

Remove a solid or static background, refine edges and stack multiple keys.

Select a video, image or recording clip and open the Effects inspector. Remove solid background samples the source border at the playhead and adds a color key. Use Screen color to select a different color. The color key, the background reference key and the luma key work without any AI model, in preview and export.

CorridorKey plugin

For hard green and blue screen shots, with hair, motion blur and see-through edges, CorridorKey from Corridor Digital is available as a plugin. Open Plugins and choose CorridorKey. It explains what is downloaded (Python, PyTorch, the program and two model files, 2 to 8 GB depending on the computer), what it needs (Apple Silicon or an NVIDIA graphics card) and its licence, and nothing is downloaded until you accept. It is slow, seconds per frame, and makes a new file with transparency. Remove Background in Design is something else: it cuts out people and objects in still images, with a small separate model.

Remove static background estimates a background reference from nine source frames. Keep the camera and lighting fixed. The subject must move enough to reveal the background in most samples. A stationary subject may become part of the reference. For better control, add a Background reference key, put the playhead on a frame with the subject out of shot, and choose Capture clean background.

Open Advanced for additional controls:

  • Recover fine detail restores fractional coverage in hair and fine edges. Increase gradually; dark patches of background can also return.
  • Preserve source shadows retains darker areas of the screen as translucent shadows. Select a bright, unshadowed Screen color. Shadow color range controls how close the shadow’s hue must be to that sample. These controls cannot distinguish a shadow from foreground with the same screen colour; use a mask where needed.
  • Recover edge color reduces screen colour contamination in translucent edge pixels. Remove color spill reduces the dominant screen channel.
  • Matte black/white set the transparent and opaque limits. Matte gamma adjusts intermediate coverage; values above 1 strengthen translucent detail.
  • Shrink / expand edge adjusts the outline; positive values shrink the foreground. Edge feather softens the boundary. Invert matte reverses the retained areas.
  • View switches between Composite and the black-and-white matte. Keep Black & white matte selected to export a reusable matte as video or with Export Frame. White retains the foreground, black removes the background, and grey represents partial coverage. Choose Composite to export the keyed picture instead. Exports include the other visible layers and subsequent effects, just as in the viewer; hide them if you need only the matte.

Fine-detail, shadow and edge-colour recovery belong to Color key. Gamma and the other matte controls also work with Background reference key and Luma key. The new recovery controls default to zero, so existing project keys keep their previous settings.

Effects run from top to bottom. Use the arrows to reorder, duplicate a key to remove another color, or switch an instance off to compare. Blur, saturation, contrast and opacity can also be added to the stack. Existing color correction and clip effects follow the stack. Masks can remove unwanted areas afterward. Stack parameters are currently static.

On recordings, choose Camera or Screen before adding an effect. Preview and export share the same renderer. Automatic background estimation is designed for a locked camera; it is not person segmentation or AI foreground reconstruction. Complex footage may need an external AI processor.

Expressions

Add automatic motion such as wiggle, pulse or continuous rotation to a value without setting keyframes.

On this pageWhich values can have an expressionAdd an expressionPresetsSettingsHow expressions combine with keyframesSee, change and remove an expressionCopying and groupsTips

An expression animates a value for you. Instead of placing many keyframes to make a title shake, a logo spin or a light flicker, you choose a preset and set how strong and how fast it is. Expressions are presets with settings, not code you type.

Which values can have an expression

Every numeric value in the inspector that has a keyframe diamond can have an expression: position, scale, rotation, opacity, crop, effect amounts, color values, pattern background settings, volume and so on. Values without a diamond, and the layout value of split screens, cannot.

Add an expression

  1. Select the clip and find the value in the inspector.
  2. Hold Alt/Option and move the pointer over the value's name or its diamond. The pointer changes to an arrow with a red ƒx badge when the value supports expressions.
  3. Click while holding Alt/Option. The expression controls open under the value.
  4. Choose a preset in the Expression menu and adjust its settings.
  5. Click Done to close the controls.

You can also reach the name with Tab and press Alt/Option and Enter or Space. The playhead must be inside the clip to Alt-click the diamond; the name works at any time.

Presets

  • Wiggle: smooth random movement around the value, up and down. A new random target is reached once per cycle.
  • Oscillate: moves smoothly back and forth, like a sine wave, between the value minus Amount and the value plus Amount.
  • Pulse: grows from the value up to the value plus Amount and back, once per cycle.
  • Rotation: continuous rotation. Only offered for rotation values. Amount is degrees per second.
  • Drift: a steady change that keeps going. Amount is units per second, for example pixels per second for position.
  • Bounce: a repeating hop above the value, like a ball bouncing on the floor.
  • Ramp: rises from the value to the value plus Amount and jumps back, once per cycle.
  • Flicker: jumps to a new random value between the value minus Amount and the value plus Amount at every cycle, with no smoothing.

Use a negative Amount to turn the direction around, for example a Pulse that shrinks instead of grows, or a Rotation that turns counterclockwise.

Settings

  • Enabled: turns the expression off without removing it, so you can compare with and without.
  • Amount: how far the value moves, in the same unit as the value itself (pixels, percent, degrees and so on). For Rotation and Drift it is the change per second.
  • Cycles / second: how fast the motion repeats. 2 means two cycles every second; 0.5 means one cycle every two seconds. For Rotation and Drift this is called Speed multiplier and multiplies Amount.
  • Phase (°): where in the cycle the motion starts. 360° is one full cycle, so 180° starts halfway. Give two clips the same preset with different phases to keep them out of step.
  • Random Seed: only for Wiggle and Flicker. Clips with the same seed and settings move the same way; change the seed for a different random pattern.

New expressions start with a moderate Amount for the value, a speed of 1 and a phase of 0. Rotation starts at 45 degrees per second.

How expressions combine with keyframes

The expression is added on top of the value. Without keyframes it moves around the number in the inspector. With keyframes it moves around the keyframed animation, so you can for example animate a title across the screen with two keyframes and let Wiggle add a shake as it travels.

The number field and the slider still edit the base value, and the diamond still adds keyframes to it. The viewer, playback and export show the result with the expression added. The result stays inside the value's normal range: an opacity cannot go above 100% or below 0%, for example. The Rotation preset is not limited.

Time is counted from the start of the clip, so the motion moves with the clip when you move it on the timeline. Trimming the start of the clip keeps the motion in place, as with keyframes. Clip speed and speed ramps are taken into account.

See, change and remove an expression

  • A value with an active expression has a red diamond, and a ƒx link with the preset name under the value. Click the link to open the controls again. A turned off expression shows (disabled) after the name.
  • The diamond's tooltip shows the active preset.
  • Remove Expression, or None in the Expression menu, removes the expression and keeps the base value and keyframes.
  • Reset (the arrow at the end of the row) removes the expression, the keyframes and returns the value to its default.

Copying and groups

Expressions are saved with the project and used in exports. Copy and Paste Attributes copies them with the other attributes, and Remove Attributes removes them. With several clips selected, an expression you add or change in the inspector is applied to all of them.

When a clip follows a track in Live mode, the track controls position and rotation (and scale if Scale is on), so expressions on those values are not used while it follows. Use Keyframes mode if you want a wiggle on top of the tracked motion.

Tips

  • A small Wiggle on Position (Amount 3 to 8, about 2 cycles per second) gives a handheld camera feel.
  • Pulse on Scale at 1 cycle per second makes a logo or button breathe.
  • Flicker on Opacity with a small Amount and high speed looks like an old film or a faulty light.
  • Rotation on a pattern background's symbol rotation gives a slowly turning background.

  • Words in [brackets] or (parentheses) are shown dimmed, as directions.

Choose Paragraphs, Sentences or Lines to decide how much is one take. Sentences suit dense or technical text, paragraphs suit storytelling.

The line you are reading is highlighted and kept a third down the window, like on a teleprompter. Click any line to go there. Prompter view (M) hides the side panel and shows only the script in large text, and the mirror button flips it for a prompter with a beam splitter glass. Change the text size with + and -.

Recording and retakes

Press R to record the line and R again to keep the take. The studio goes to the next line, so you can read the whole script with R alone. Space plays the timeline, and clicking a line moves the playhead to where its clip is, or where it will go.

  • Shift R discards what you are recording and starts the line again right away. Use it the moment you stumble.
  • Esc discards the take that is recording.
  • Backspace marks the last take as not usable and goes back to its line, ready for a new try.
  • Up and Down move between lines, Shift with the arrows moves between sections, and Shift N goes to the next line without a take.
  • P plays the chosen take of the line, and F stars it.

Each line keeps all its takes. The latest take is used, unless you star one or click another one in the list. A crossed out take is never used.

Count in waits one or three seconds before recording. Cue last line plays the end of the take before, so the new line matches its tone and pace.

Clean edits without work

Each take is trimmed where your voice starts and ends, with a little air before and air after. Soft starts such as a breath or an s are kept. Adjust a take with the scissors in the list if needed. Takes that clip or where no voice was found are marked.

The room tone is used as the noise floor when the studio listens for your voice, and it fills the pauses between lines on the timeline, so the voice never drops to digital silence.

Aim for peaks between -12 and -6 dB on the meter. The studio records 24-bit WAV without echo cancellation, noise suppression or automatic gain. Choose the input channel if your interface has the microphone on input 2.

Talking head

Turn on Camera to record video with each take. The takes are WebM files with the picture and the sound, and they are trimmed the same way.

Sound: Studio Voice out of the box

Every person starts with Studio Voice: rumble and light noise removed, warmth and presence, soft S sounds and an even level, so it sounds like voice over straight away. Adjust it under Sound for in the side panel. It works like Audio Effects on a clip: add effects or templates, turn each one on or off, change the order, adjust it or remove it. Volume sets the level for all of the person's takes, and Reset to Studio Voice starts over. You hear the changes in the studio timeline while it plays, and they go with the clips when you stitch.

A deeper voice: the Voice templates Warm Narrator, Deep Voice and Trailer Voice make a voice deeper, bigger and closer the way a studio does it, without the artificial sound of strong pitch. They use Voice Character, one effect with the controls that matter: Depth (body in the low end), Presence (clarity), Density (compression), Warmth (soft saturation) and a small Pitch range for a touch more depth. Add a template from the Audio Effects browser and adjust it under Sound for. It works on recordings and AI voices, also on existing takes, and follows the clips into Edit and export.

Click Add Person to add another voice on its own track, with their own takes and their own sound. If the script has roles, the new person gets the next role name. Double click the name to rename.

The studio timeline

At the bottom of the studio, the takes you keep are laid out in script order, the way they will be stitched. Space plays from the yellow line when the pointer is over the timeline, or from the playhead otherwise. Click the ruler or an empty spot to move the playhead, and drag to scrub. Hover to skim, pinch or Cmd/Ctrl scroll to zoom, and drag the top edge of the timeline to make it taller.

The timeline toolbar has the same three tools and shortcuts as Edit: the magnet toggles Snapping (N) to clip edges and the playhead, Skimming (S) toggles the yellow line that follows the pointer, and the headphones toggle Audio skimming (Shift S). Audio skimming is available while Skimming is on. These switches share their settings with Edit. Snapping works when moving or trimming clips, adjusting pauses, placing the playhead and cutting. Skimming pauses during playback and recording. With Playback from skimmer enabled in Settings, Space starts at the yellow line; otherwise it starts at the playhead.

With the select tool (A), drag a clip sideways to move it and all the clips after it. The new pause is filled with the selected room tone when Room tone in pauses is on. You can move the first clip too, to leave room tone before the voice starts. Drag the end of an existing pause to make it shorter or longer.

Right click a clip for Fade In, Fade Out, Split Here and Delete. The left panel has two collapsible groups, Templates first and Effects below, with icons and search. Click a group heading to collapse or expand it, or scroll down through both groups. The studio remembers which groups are open. Point at a card for its description. Drag an effect or template onto a track or one of its clips to apply it to the whole person track, including its room tone. You can also right click a card and choose Add to track. Drop a fade onto a clip for that clip, or onto the track header or an empty spot on the track for all its clips. Adjust the track effects under Sound in the right panel. Click the track header to choose the track that receives effects when you click a card. Use the left and right panel icons in the top bar to show or hide each panel; the studio remembers your choices.

Press B for the cut tool, shown as scissors in both Edit and Voice Over Studio. The scissors pointer and the cut line show where the split will land. Click to make two separate clips, each with its own trim, volume, volume keyframes and fades. Press A or Esc to return to selecting, then click a piece to edit or move it independently. Delete removes only the selected clip and closes up the following material. The original recording stays in the take list, and each cut can be undone on its own. The yellow line in each clip is its volume: drag it, or Option/Alt click to add keyframes, like in Edit. Trim to Voice finds where the voice starts and ends in every clip. AI Clean Voice under Sound removes steady noise from the takes and their room tone, and the waveforms show the result.

Click a clip or a piece of room tone to select it, Shift or Cmd/Ctrl click to select more, or Cmd/Ctrl A to select everything. Delete removes the selected material; Restore Deleted brings it back. Cmd/Ctrl Z undoes changes, and Shift Cmd/Ctrl Z redoes them. A whole drag is one undo step. The studio keeps its own undo history, separate from Edit.

Click a clip to select it. The script jumps to its line.

  • Drag the yellow edges to trim the start or the end. The clips after it move along.
  • Drag the round handles at the top to fade in and out.
  • The toolbar under the timeline always stays in place. With no clip selected, its fields are empty and disabled. Select a clip to adjust Volume, Fade in, Fade out, Pause after, and exact Start and End. The yellow line, clip badge and Volume field show the combined clip and track volume, including changes under Sound. Changing the toolbar volume adjusts only that clip. With keyframes, the field follows the playhead and adjusts the whole volume curve; the diamond button removes the keyframes while keeping the current level. Normalize sets the combined peak to -3 dB.
  • Delete (or Backspace) removes the clip from the timeline. The takes are kept, the line is crossed out in the script, and clicking deleted on the line or Restore Deleted brings it back. Recording a new take of the line also brings it back.
  • Double click a clip to hear only that clip.

Everything you adjust here goes with the clips when you stitch.

Space between clips

Pauses above the studio timeline sets the default space between clips: after a sentence in the same paragraph, after a paragraph and before a section (a new heading or scene). The script decides which one is used. The same settings are in Settings → Editing → Voice Over Studio, together with the air kept before and after the voice when a take is trimmed.

To change the space after one clip, select it and change Pause after. Default takes it back to the default, and Use for all makes that pause the new default for all pauses of the same kind. Clear own pauses in Pauses takes every clip back to the defaults.

Stitch to the timeline

Send to Edit (Cmd or Ctrl and Return in the studio) stitches the chosen takes in script order, with room tone in the pauses, at the playhead or at the end, and opens Edit so you can keep working. The pause after each line follows the script: a short pause after a sentence, a longer one after a paragraph and the longest before a new section. Change the pauses under Stitch.

  • Voice takes go on one audio track under the picture, grouped so they move together, with room tone in the pauses.
  • Talking head takes go above all other video tracks, or on V1 when there is room for them. Existing clips keep their timing. Audio-only takes stay on audio tracks, even when the same person also has video takes. With one person and only video takes, the clips are cut back to back and the pause uses the take's own sound.

Turn on Subtitles (next to Send to Edit, or Subtitles from the script under Stitch) to also get subtitles with the text from the script, timed to where each clip lands. Long lines are split into readable pieces. Fix them in the Captions tab like any other captions. After retakes, press Send to Edit again. The clips from the last stitch are replaced, so the timeline always has the best takes. The takes are also in the Media browser, named after the line, so you can use them by hand.

The script is added to the project story, so the AI Agent can use it to match the takes to the script.

Ducking Music Under Speech

Lower the music automatically while someone speaks, and bring it back in the pauses.

Select one or more music clips and choose Clip → Duck Music Under Speech, or open the Ducking section in the Audio tab and click Duck Under Speech.

Reccut listens to all other audio on the timeline: camera sound, microphones, voice over and recordings. Wherever someone speaks, the music fades down, and in the pauses it fades back up. Muted, disabled and soloed clips count as they sound when you play.

The result is ordinary volume keyframes on the music, so you see the dips on the clip, hear them at once and get the same result in the export. Drag a point to adjust a dip, or delete the points you do not want. Undo removes everything in one step. Ducking replaces the volume keyframes the music already had; the level at the start of the clip is the level the music returns to.

  • Lower by: how far the music goes down while someone speaks. 12 dB is a good start; 18 to 24 dB for quiet narration.
  • Fade down and Fade up: how quickly the music goes down before the speech and comes back after it.
  • Bridge pauses: pauses shorter than this keep the music down, so it does not pump between words.

The settings are remembered for the next time. Speech is found from the waveforms, so wait until new clips have finished analyzing.

Video Scopes

Measure the displayed image with luma and RGB waveforms, RGB parade, histogram and vectorscope.

Open View → Video Scopes (Ctrl+7), or click the scope icon beside the viewer's playback controls. Scopes are optional and off by default. They follow the image displayed in the viewer after grading, LUTs, effects and compositing.

Choose Four scopes or one larger view:

  • Luma waveform: brightness from 0 to 100%, preserving the image's left-to-right position.
  • RGB waveform: the three color channels overlaid at the same image positions.
  • RGB parade: separate red, green and blue waveforms for comparing channel levels and color casts.
  • Histogram: the number of pixels at each RGB and luma code value from 0 to 255. The pale trace is luma.
  • Vectorscope: hue direction and chroma strength around a neutral center, with 75% color-bar targets and an optional approximate skin-hue guide.

Gain changes trace brightness only. Log switches the histogram between logarithmic and linear counts. Hold freezes the scopes without pausing the video. Resume to follow the viewer again. Drag the panel's lower-right corner to resize it. Close with the cross or the same menu command. Your layout and display settings are remembered.

The scopes read a sampled 8-bit SDR viewer signal, not raw camera data or HDR luminance in nits. LOG and HDR sources are measured after their conversion to the viewer. Luma uses Rec.709 coefficients. The skin guide is a reference direction, not a target for every skin tone or light source.

RGB at 255 is the fraction of sampled pixels with at least one channel at the upper code value. It does not by itself prove overexposure in the original source. Sampling and reduced preview resolution can miss small details or short peaks.

Scopes update up to eight times per second, reuse unchanged frames, and stop sampling when held or hidden. Closing the panel removes its sampler. Scopes and guide labels do not appear in exports. They are hidden during full-screen playback.

Editing

Select, Copy, Paste and Undo

Choose what an edit affects, reuse clips and recover from a change.

On this pageCopy or move clipsUndo and redo

Click a clip to select it. Use Shift-click to add or remove clips from a selection. Select All (Ctrl+A) selects all timeline clips, or all media when the browser is in use. Deselect All (Ctrl+Shift+A) clears the selection. Select Clip at Playhead (C) selects the clip at the current time.

Copy or move clips

  1. Select the clips you want to reuse and choose Copy (Ctrl+C), or Cut (Ctrl+X) to remove them and put them on the clipboard.
  2. Put the playhead where you want the pasted content.
  3. Choose Paste (Ctrl+V). Play across the pasted area and check the surrounding timing.

Use Paste Effects or Paste Attributes when you want to copy a look or audio treatment instead of creating another clip. Grouped clips can select together; Option/Alt-click selects a single member.

Undo and redo

Undo (Ctrl+Z) reverses the previous edit. Redo (Ctrl+Shift+Z) reapplies an undone edit until a new change replaces that redo history. For an older saved state, open Project History. In the AI Agent window, Undo this request can revert all timeline changes from the last request.

When a text field has focus, Cut, Copy, Paste, Select All, Undo and Redo act on the text. Click the timeline before using them on clips. In the Help window they edit or copy Help text rather than the project behind it.

Getting Started

Your First Video: Record, Edit and Export

A complete practice project, from an empty timeline to a finished video.

On this pageCreate a projectAdd your footageMake two clean cutsAdd a title and check the soundExport the result

You need a screen or a short video file and a folder where Reccut can save the project. This example makes a short tutorial with a title and a clean ending.

Create a project

  1. Choose File → New Project and name it Practice Tutorial. Choose a location with enough free space for your recordings.
  2. Keep the automatic format if you want the first video to set the sequence size and frame rate.
  3. Reccut creates the project folder. Changes to the edit are saved automatically.

Add your footage

  1. Open Record, choose the screen and microphone, and use Test to check your voice. Record about 20 seconds, then stop.
  2. Open Edit. The recording is in the media library. If the timeline was empty it is added automatically.
  3. Alternatively, choose File → Import Media, select a video, and choose Copy to project folder. Select it in the library and use Append (E).
  4. If Sequence Settings opens, confirm the format. Press Space to play; press it again to pause.

Make two clean cuts

  1. Click the timeline ruler where your useful content starts. Select the clip and choose Blade (Ctrl+B).
  2. Click the unwanted beginning and press Delete. The magnetic storyline closes the gap.
  3. Repeat at the end. Use Select (A) to drag a clip edge when you only need a smaller adjustment.
  4. Choose Fit Timeline (Shift+Z) if the useful part is outside the view.

Add a title and check the sound

  1. Put the playhead near the start and open Titles. Drag a title above the video.
  2. Select the title and change its text in the inspector. Drag its edges to set how long it stays visible.
  3. Select the video and open Audio. Adjust its volume while listening. Red levels mean the sound is clipping.
  4. Play the entire edit. Check the beginning, every cut, the title, and the ending.

Export the result

  1. Choose File → Export (Ctrl+E).
  2. Use MP4 H.264 for this example, name the file Practice Tutorial, and start the export.
  3. Watch its progress in Background tasks. The output is normally saved in the project's Exports folder.
  4. Open the finished file and watch it once before sharing it.

Expected result: a video with its empty beginning and ending removed, a readable title, and audible speech. You can reopen the project and continue editing without changing the original recording.

Set Up the AI Agent

Connect Google Gemini, Claude, OpenAI or your own AI service in a few minutes, and learn what goes in each field.

On this pageThe quickest way: Google Gemini (free to start)Claude or OpenAIUse another provider or your own serverWhat goes in each fieldTest the connectionIf something goes wrongKeys and project data

The AI Agent edits your project when you describe what you want. It needs an AI service to think with. You connect the service once, and Reccut remembers it.

What you need: an account with an AI provider and an API key from it. An API key is a secret code that lets Reccut use your account, a bit like a password made for one app. Prefer to run the AI on your own computer instead? See Use a Local AI Model.

The quickest way: Google Gemini (free to start)

  1. Click AI Agent in the top bar. The first time, Reccut shows Set up the AI Agent.
  2. Choose Google Gemini (marked Recommended).
  3. Click Open Google Gemini and create an API key. Your browser opens Google AI Studio. Sign in with your Google account.
  4. Click Create API key in Google AI Studio, then copy the key.
  5. Back in Reccut, paste the key in the field and click Connect.
  6. Reccut finds the best model for you and tests the connection. When it says The AI Agent is ready, you can start.

On Google's free tier, Google may use what you send to improve its products. Add billing in Google AI Studio to avoid that and to get higher limits.

Claude or OpenAI

Both work the same way as Gemini, but you pay per use, so the account needs a payment method or credits first.

  • Claude: choose Claude, click the link to open the Anthropic Console, add credits under Billing, then create a key under API Keys.
  • OpenAI: choose OpenAI, click the link to open the OpenAI platform, add a payment method under Billing, then create a key under API keys.

Paste the key and click Connect. You only pay for what the agent uses. Long edits with many clips cost more than short ones.

Use another provider or your own server

Use Settings → AI → AI Agent to connect anything the setup does not list: Mistral, OpenRouter, Groq, xAI, DeepSeek, a company gateway, or a model server on your network. Click ? next to a field to see what goes in it.

  1. Choose a Provider. If yours is not listed, choose Other server or gateway.
  2. For Other server or gateway, choose the API format and enter the API base URL from your provider's documentation.
  3. Paste your API key. Leave it empty for a local server that does not need one.
  4. Click Fetch models and pick a model in Model ID.
  5. Turn Model can see images on or off to match the model.
  6. Click Save AI settings, then Open chat.

What goes in each field

  • Provider: the company or program that runs the AI model. Choosing a provider fills in its address for you.
  • API format: only for Other server or gateway. Most services use OpenAI-compatible, including vLLM, Jan, LocalAI, LiteLLM and Azure. Choose Anthropic Messages only for a Claude-compatible server.
  • API base URL: only for Other server or gateway. The address of the service's API, usually ending in /v1, for example https://api.example.com/v1. It is not the address of a chat web page, and it does not end in /chat/completions. Use https, or http for a server on this computer or your local network.
  • API key: the secret code from your provider. It is stored encrypted on this computer and only sent to that provider. When the field says Key saved, leave it empty to keep the saved key. Changing provider or address removes the old key.
  • Model ID: the exact name of the model, as the provider's API uses it. Click Fetch models to choose from the models your key or server can use, rather than typing a name. The model must support tool calling, which is how the agent makes edits. Larger, newer models edit better.
  • Model can see images: turn on if the model accepts pictures. The agent then checks its own work by looking at frames from your video, and you can attach reference images. Turn off for text-only models.
  • Remove key: deletes the saved key from this computer.

Test the connection

Open the AI Agent window and send: “Describe the current timeline. Do not change anything.” The agent should read the project and reply. Then try “Add a title saying Welcome at the playhead.” Check the result in the viewer. Use Undo this request to remove that request's changes.

If something goes wrong

Reccut explains the problem in plain words and shows the provider's code in brackets.

  • The API key was not accepted (401): copy the whole key again, without spaces, and check that it belongs to the provider you chose.
  • The account has no credits (402) or the usage limit is reached (429): add a payment method or credits in your provider account, or wait a minute.
  • No access to the model (403): choose another model with Fetch models, or check the key's permissions.
  • The model or address was not found (404): check Model ID and API base URL.
  • Could not reach …: check your internet connection. For a local server, check that it is running and has a model loaded.

See Troubleshoot the AI Connection for more.

Keys and project data

Keys use the operating system's encrypted credential storage when available. Otherwise they are kept only in memory for the current session. Remove key deletes the key. Changing provider or base URL removes the old key, so enter a new one for the new connection.

The agent can send your messages, attached images, clip names, timings, captions, locally generated transcripts, look measurements and rendered frames to the configured provider. Original video and audio files are not uploaded by the chat. Provider processing is external; read its terms before using private material. Speech models for transcripts are optional downloads in Settings → AI.

Use a Local AI Model

Connect Reccut to Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp or a compatible local server.

On this pageConnect automaticallyConnect manuallyIf it connects but cannot edit

Install and start a model server separately, then load a model that supports tool calling. Reccut does not install or host the language model. Downloading a model needs internet access; a running model on this computer can then handle the chat locally. Reccut's speech models for subtitles are separate optional downloads in Settings → AI.

Connect automatically

  1. Start your local server and load a tool-capable model. Wait until the model is ready.
  2. Click AI Agent in the top bar and choose On this computer (the last option) in the setup.
  3. Reccut looks for Ollama and LM Studio. Choose one of the models it found and choose Use.
  4. If it finds no models, check the server and choose Look again. Use manual settings for a different port or llama.cpp.

Connect manually

Open Settings → AI → AI Agent. The preset addresses in Reccut are:

  • Ollama (local): http://localhost:11434/v1
  • LM Studio (local): http://localhost:1234/v1
  • llama.cpp server (local): http://localhost:8080/v1
  1. Choose the preset that matches your server. These presets use an OpenAI-compatible API format.
  2. Leave API key empty for a local server that does not require authentication.
  3. Choose Fetch models, select the exact loaded model ID, and set Model can see images to match its capabilities.
  4. Choose Save AI settings. Open chat and ask “List the clips in this project without making changes.”

For another address, port or a server on your network, choose Other server or gateway and enter the actual base URL. A network server processes requests on that machine, rather than on this computer.

If it connects but cannot edit

A model that can answer ordinary chat may still be unable to call the editing tools. Check tool-calling support in both the model and the server. Turn image support off for a text-only model. Try a small request such as “Read the timeline and tell me its duration.” before requesting a complete edit.

Local performance depends on model size, available memory and your hardware. Start with short, specific tasks. If the server stops, start it and load the model again before retrying in Reccut.

AI Editing: Prompts and Worked Examples

Give the agent a clear brief, point to clips, review its work and undo a whole request.

On this pageMake a tutorialEdit from a script or briefCaptions and translationColour, graphics and audioControl and review a request

Set up a model first and import your footage. The agent operates on the current project and can inspect its library, timeline, transcripts and rendered frames. Select clips or move the playhead before referring to “the selected clips” or “the clip under the playhead”.

Make a tutorial

  1. Import your talking-head and screen recordings. Name them so the content is easy to recognise.
  2. Send “Make a two-minute tutorial for first-time users from my narration and screen recordings. Remove failed takes and long pauses. Keep the useful steps in order. Add a title at the start.”
  3. The agent can transcribe speech, inspect contact sheets and scene changes, and construct an edit. Long clips and the first transcription can take time.
  4. Play through every cut. Check the chosen takes and whether the screen matches the narration. Ask for a specific correction, such as “Keep the explanation after the second click; it was cut too early.”

Edit from a script or brief

Use Attach… for PDF, Typeloom, Word, Final Draft, Fountain, RTF or text documents. Attach storyboard images as images. For example: “Use the attached script. Pick the clearest take of each line, follow its scene order, and aim for 90 seconds.” The project can retain the story brief, documents and paper edit. A Typeloom project preserves binder order and scene information.

Without a brief, the agent may ask about the audience, length and tone. Answer those questions before expecting a complete story edit.

Captions and translation

Try “Create English captions, then translate them into Finnish. Keep names unchanged and use concise sentences.” Speech recognition runs locally; caption text and transcripts can be sent to the configured chat provider for translation. Review names, punctuation and timing. The agent aims for readable lines and non-overlapping captions; a successful request is not a guarantee that every word is correct.

Colour, graphics and audio

  • Match clips: “Correct exposure and white balance on the selected interview clips and make them match.” Review skin tones and highlights.
  • Match a reference: attach an image using Image…, paste it or drop it on the message field. Ask: “Use this image as a colour reference for the selected clip.”
  • Graphics: “Make a five-second intro saying Getting Started on a dark blue background.” The agent can render a graphic as media and place it in the project.
  • Chart: “Create an animated bar chart for Q1 120, Q2 180, Q3 240 and place it at 0:45.” Check values, spelling and its timing.
  • Sound: “Put the music on its own lane at −15 dB and fade it out over the final two seconds.” Listen and adjust the balance for your footage.

Control and review a request

Stop ends a running request between steps; it does not remove changes already made. Undo this request reverts the edit changes associated with that request. Ordinary Undo steps back through individual changes. Rendered media, installed plugins and external generation charges are not erased by undoing timeline edits.

The agent cannot currently create voice-over or music, or start an export. Export the reviewed edit yourself. An AI video request uses the separately configured Higgsfield plugin and may involve a paid generation.

Troubleshoot the AI Connection

Resolve missing models, rejected keys, unavailable tools, image errors and stalled requests.

On this pageKey rejected or access deniedNo models, model not found or wrong URLConnection refused on a local modelAnswers arrive but the timeline never changesImages rejected or a text-only model failsA request is slow or fails partway through

Read the error message first: it says what went wrong and what to check. Then open Settings → AI → AI Agent and check Provider, Model ID and API base URL. Click ? next to a field to see what goes in it. Try Fetch models. If that fails, resolve the connection before retrying a large editing request.

Key rejected or access denied

Check that the key belongs to the selected provider and has API access to the chosen model. Re-enter it after changing the provider or URL. Provider credits, permissions and usage limits are managed outside Reccut. Do not paste a key into the chat or an attached document.

No models, model not found or wrong URL

Use Fetch models and copy the exact ID. A model name seen in a consumer chat app may differ from the API model ID. For a custom connection, check the API format and the base URL, including any required version path. Do not append a chat or completion route to the base URL.

Connection refused on a local model

Start the local server, enable its API service if needed, and load a model. Confirm that the address and port match Reccut's preset. If your server uses a different port, use Other server or gateway. A server on another computer needs that computer's network address, not localhost.

Answers arrive but the timeline never changes

Ask for a small, explicit edit and check the model's tool-calling support. The server must expose tool calling too. Questions such as “How do I add a title?” ask for help; “Add a title saying Welcome” asks for an edit. Check that footage is imported and the intended sequence is open.

Images rejected or a text-only model fails

Turn off Model can see images, save settings and retry without image attachments. For colour matching or visual inspection, choose a model and server that accept images.

A request is slow or fails partway through

Look at the chat's tool steps and Background tasks. Optional speech-model downloads and long transcriptions can take time. Check that a local server is still running and has enough memory. A provider may impose rate or quota limits. Use Stop, review changes already made, and retry a smaller task. Use Undo this request if you want to discard its timeline changes.

Install, Use and Share Plugins

Open plugin tools, choose defaults, generate video and review plugin packages.

On this pageApply a processor pluginInstall a plugin fileGenerate a shot with HiggsfieldShare or remove a plugin

Open Plugins in the toolbar to see the available tools. Plugins open in small movable tool windows inside the application. Settings → Plugins lists their settings, sharing and removal actions. The built-in Higgsfield AI Video plugin is supplied with Reccut; its account is configured separately from the editing agent.

Apply a processor plugin

  1. Select a video clip and open a plugin from Plugins.
  2. Adjust the plugin's controls. Choose Apply to selected clip.
  3. Processing runs as a background task. Wait for completion, then inspect the result in the viewer.
  4. The settings gear opens its defaults in Settings. These defaults also apply when the agent uses that plugin, unless it supplies other values.

Install a plugin file

Double-click a .reccut-plugin file or use Install plugin file…. Read the plugin description, parameters and What it runs. If you trust the code, accept the installation terms and choose Install plugin. An update keeps the plugin's identity instead of creating a second copy. A plugin can run code with your computer's permissions; the installation review is part of the application workflow.

You can ask the agent: “Make a plugin that sharpens soft footage, with an adjustable strength slider.” It presents a proposed processor for review. Generating a proposal does not install it automatically.

Generate a shot with Higgsfield

  1. Open Higgsfield AI Video and its settings. Configure your account/key and available models.
  2. Describe the shot. Optionally use the frame at the playhead as a start image.
  3. Check the model, duration and estimated price. Confirm the generation when prompted.
  4. Wait for the generation, then inspect the imported result. Your provider account handles billing.

Generation sends the prompt and any supplied start image to the video service. It is distinct from local chat or a local video processor. Undoing an edit does not cancel a generation charge.

Share or remove a plugin

In Settings → Plugins, choose Share…, enter the author information, review the sharing terms and export a .reccut-plugin file. Someone else can review and install that file. Choose Remove to uninstall a user plugin. The built-in Higgsfield tool can be configured but cannot be removed.

External Video Processors and Compatibility

Understand local processing, timing requirements, generated files and unsupported plugin formats.

On this pageProcess a clipBackground removal with an external toolIf processing fails

An external processor is a local tool connected through a Reccut JSON manifest. It can be a generated FFmpeg/Python plugin or a manifest for a trusted executable installed separately. Install the executable, model files, runtime and licence required by that tool before using it.

Process a clip

  1. Import a video and select its timeline clip.
  2. Open Effects → External processing, or open the installed plugin tool from Plugins.
  3. Choose the processor and its parameters, then apply it. The processor receives the full source, rather than only the visible trimmed part.
  4. Wait for the background task. The result is imported and applied to the selected clip. Original media and audio remain available.

The processed result must preserve source timing and be a readable video file. Changing duration, frame rate interpretation or the start offset in a processor can make the edit point at the wrong source frames. Test on a short source first.

Background removal with an external tool

For difficult foreground extraction, use a processor that writes video with an alpha channel, or process in a standalone application and import its result. For green and blue screens with hair, motion blur and see-through edges, CorridorKey is available as an optional plugin under Plugins. Reccut cannot load native OFX or Adobe plugins directly.

If processing fails

Read the failed task's message. Check the executable path, installed runtime and models, input access and output format. Cancel through Background tasks if the process is no longer useful. Choose the sources

  2. Select the camera and microphone you intend to use. Disconnecting a device can make its saved selection unavailable.
  3. Turn on System audio if the recording needs sound from the computer. Keep it off if you only need your voice.
  4. For part of a display, choose Area, draw the box, and choose Use Area. Only the selected area is stored.

Grant permissions

On macOS, allow screen recording, camera and microphone access in the system's Privacy & Security settings. If you use the Keystroke Add-on, macOS also asks to allow Input Monitoring for Reccut. If the preview is black, check the selected source and screen permission before changing export settings.

Set the microphone level

  1. Speak at the volume you will use. Watch the meter and use Test for a four-second recording and playback.
  2. Start with input gain at 0 dB. Aim for clear speech without red clipping; peaks around −18 to −6 dBFS are a useful starting point.
  3. Open Microphone mixer for input/output meters, EQ, low cut and compression. Try Flat, Clear speech, Warm voice or Bright voice and compare with Test.

Make a rehearsal

Record ten seconds, pause and resume once, change a layout, then stop. Open it in Edit and check the voice, screen, camera and timing. Choose a countdown and the floating controls before starting the real take. Keep enough free disk space in the project's recording location.

The active project's Recordings folder contains the take's media and recording metadata. Screen, camera, audio and recorded events are retained for editing. The optional automatic MP4 is a rendered version; keep the recording folder if you want to change its layout and overlays later.

Pause without leaving a gap

Use Pause on the floating controls, or the configured recording modifier with 9. Repeat to resume. Paused time is removed, and recorded events follow the shortened time. Keyboard control from another app is enabled by default while the Record room is open. You can turn off Record → Shortcuts → Allow shortcuts in other apps to limit shortcuts to Reccut outside a recording. The floating controls work regardless of this setting.

Stop and wait for saving

Use Stop or the start/stop shortcut. Allow saving to finish before moving or renaming the recording folder. If automatic rendering is on, a finished MP4 is made in the background. You can still edit the original recording.

After a crash

  1. Reopen Reccut. It checks for interrupted recordings and offers recovery.
  2. Choose the take to recover. Reccut imports what it can read, with event metadata up to its last checkpoint.
  3. Play the recovered beginning and ending, and check audio and camera synchronisation before editing.
  4. Use discard only when you no longer need that interrupted take.

Recovery can lose the final unwritten portion; it cannot recreate data the disk never received. Project History restores edit decisions, while recording recovery repairs an interrupted media take. Use Package Project to collect a completed project's media for backup.

Choose the working format

Editing

Animate a Zoom with Keyframes

A practical example using scale, easing, a hold and a return to the original framing.

Use a video or still image with enough resolution for a small zoom. Select it and open the Video inspector. Animation affects the selected clip; it does not change the source file.

  1. Put the playhead one second after the clip starts. Set Scale to 100% and click its diamond to save a keyframe.
  2. Move one second further and set Scale to 120%. Add or confirm the second keyframe. Change Position if the subject needs to stay centred.
  3. Move another second and keep Scale at 120%, adding a keyframe for the end of the hold.
  4. Move one second further and return to 100%. Play the whole animation.

Expected result: a zoom from 100% to 120%, a pause, and a return. Check the values in your UI: some controls display percentages while others display native units.

Control the motion

Right-click a keyframe on the selected clip to choose Linear, Ease In, Ease Out, Ease In and Out, or Hold. Linear moves at a constant rate. Easing makes the speed change gradually. Hold keeps a value until the next keyframe, producing a step rather than a smooth change.

Drag a keyframe to change when it happens. Use the inspector's keyframe arrows to visit the exact times. New keyframes use the default in Settings → Editing → Default keyframe interpolation; changing the default does not rewrite existing keyframes.

Animation presets are another starting point in the Video inspector. Applying a preset can replace the properties it animates. Use Undo if it replaces motion you wanted to keep.

Generate, Correct and Deliver Captions

Generate captions

  1. Open the Captions browser (Ctrl+Shift+7).
  2. Choose the speech language and an installed model. Use Manage Models to install optional models or switch the active selection. Larger models can take longer and use more resources.
  3. Choose Generate captions. Watch the background task, then inspect the caption track.
  4. Play through the result. Correct names, punctuation, speaker changes and timing in the inspector and timeline.

Import or write your own

Import SRT or WebVTT from the Captions browser. Use Add Caption (Alt+C) at the playhead to write one manually. Drag caption edges to adjust timing. Caption times stay independent when you move or delete video; explicitly group captions with video when you want them to follow it.

Example: for a line that should run from 0:05 to 0:08, place a caption there, type the line and drag its end to 0:08. Leave enough time to read it, and avoid covering an important on-screen control.

Style and translate

Adjust the sequence caption style: font, size, colour, outline, background, alignment and position. Reccut supports up to three lines, but short lines are easier to read. Clean Speech and Mix Background Music

A repeatable starting point for dialogue cleanup, component choice and music fades.

On this pagePrepare the dialogueAdd music under speechCheck the delivery

Start by choosing the right recording, then process it. A quiet microphone component plus an unintentionally enabled camera microphone can sound hollow even when neither is distorted.

Prepare the dialogue

  1. Select the video and open Audio. Enable the intended microphone component; disable duplicate camera audio if an external recording replaces it.
  2. Play an ordinary sentence and the loudest section. Set clip volume without red clipping.
  3. In Audio Effects, try Voice Clean Up or add High Pass for low rumble and Hum Removal for a steady electrical tone.
  4. Use Noise Reduction gently for steady background hiss. Compare with the effect off; aggressive reduction can affect the voice.
  5. For uneven speech, try Auto Level or Compressor, and use a Limiter when you need to catch peaks. Compare at a similar perceived volume.

Add music under speech

  1. Import the music and connect it on an audio lane below the storyline. Trim it to the edit.
  2. Lower the music while listening to the dialogue. For example, try −18 dB as a starting point, then adjust by ear.
  3. Option/Alt-click the music's volume line to add four points around a spoken section. Lower the middle pair to create a quiet section with a transition on each side.
  4. Drag the top-corner fade handles to fade music in and out. Play through the full mix and the ending.

Check the delivery

Solo can help identify noise, but restore the full mix before judging the balance. Export a short range and listen through headphones and speakers. A limiter cannot repair sound that clipped in the original microphone recording.

Export Controls: Format, Size, Quality and Captions

Understand every group in the Export room and choose settings deliberately.

On this pageSource and previewPreset and formatSize, fit and cropFrame rate and source mediaQuality and bitrateRange, captions and file

Quick Export is useful for the current edit. Choose More Options, or open Export (Ctrl+3), for the full controls. The Export room can also convert files without placing them in an edit.

Source and preview

Choose the project or a media source. Several files can be queued for conversion. The preview can show the full Source with its crop frame or the final Output framing. Preview quality in Edit does not set export resolution.

Preset and format

Presets select a useful combination that you can change. Simple mode chooses a format and codec together. Advanced mode exposes the Container, Video codec and Audio codec separately. Only compatible combinations are offered. Audio only uses its own format list.

A container is the file wrapper, such as MP4 or MOV; a codec is the compression inside it, such as H.264. Choosing MOV alone does not mean the video is ProRes. See Export Format Reference for the supported combinations.

Size, fit and crop

Set an output size or enter dimensions. The aspect lock keeps proportions while you change a dimension. Fit determines whether content is fitted with bars, filled by cropping, or stretched. Stretching can distort circles and faces.

Choose a crop aspect ratio and position it in the preview. For a portrait crop of a landscape video, move the crop over the important action and check the beginning, middle and end. Cropping the export does not reframe individual timeline clips for you.

Frame rate and source media

Keep the sequence/source frame rate unless you need a specific delivery rate. A warning identifies source-rate combinations that may stutter. Where Source media is offered, choose whether to use available optimized copies or originals. A missing source remains a problem even if the timeline project file opens.

Quality and bitrate

Higher quality or bitrate generally produces larger files. Custom quality exposes the video bitrate. Audio bitrate is separate. The estimated file size is an estimate: actual size depends on codec, content and duration. ProRes is intended for further editing and can be much larger than H.264; GIF has no sound.

Range, captions and file

Everything exports the source duration. Custom sets From and To; check that this is the range you intend. Captions can be burned in, embedded, saved separately, combined with SRT, or left out. Container/player compatibility matters for embedded tracks.

Set the file name and destination, then start export. The queue shows running, queued, finished and failed jobs. You can keep editing while it renders; each job uses the edit/settings supplied when it was started. Cancel a running or queued job from its controls.

Background Tasks, Progress and Cancellation

Track exports, analysis, optimization, captions, packaging and plugin processing.

Reccut performs long operations in background jobs. The progress control at the top of the application opens Background tasks. Exports also appear in the Export room's queue.

  • Queued means the task is waiting for a worker. Heavy jobs may run in sequence to avoid exhausting the computer.
  • Running shows the current operation and its progress when measurable.
  • Finished means the operation completed. Use any result or reveal action shown by that task.
  • Failed means the task ended with an error. Read its message before repeating the operation.

Turquoise stripes are a processing indicator, not a colour label. They can appear during stabilization, optimization or preparation of reverse media. A clip may play with its existing source while another version is being prepared.

Use Cancel on a queued or running job to stop it. Cancellation is not Undo: it does not remove edits you made before starting the task. Clear finished clears the completed entries from the list; it is not a command to delete your original media.

Example: if optimized media is queued behind an export, let the export finish or cancel the unneeded job. If a task fails because the output disk is full, free space or select another destination before starting it again.

Glossary: Editing, Colour and AI Terms

Plain-English definitions for terms used in the interface and handbook.

On this pageProjects and editingPicture and deliverySound and AI

Projects and editing

  • Project: the saved edit data and shared media library, normally inside a project folder.
  • Sequence: one timeline, with its own format and edit, inside a project.
  • Media/source: an imported recording, video, image or audio asset. A timeline clip refers to part of it.
  • Playhead: the current time shown in the viewer. Skimmer: a temporary preview position under the pointer.
  • Magnetic storyline: the main sequence of clips; trimming and deleting close up the surrounding edit.
  • Lane/track: a horizontal timeline row for connected video, audio, titles or other clips.
  • Ripple: change a duration and move following content to keep the storyline closed.
  • Roll: move a cut while keeping the pair's total duration. Slip: change source content inside a fixed clip. Slide: move a clip while adjusting its neighbours.
  • J/L cut: sound starts before or ends after its associated picture.
  • Source handles: extra source material outside a clip's in/out points, useful for transitions and trim changes.

Picture and delivery

  • Keyframe: a saved property value at a particular time. Easing: how the value changes between keyframes.
  • Mask: a shape controlling what part of a clip or effect is visible.
  • Matte: the transparency map. White is visible, black is transparent. Alpha: transparency stored with the image/video.
  • Chroma key: remove a chosen background colour. Despill: reduce background colour contamination at the edge.
  • LUT: a lookup table transforming colours. A camera LUT converts a particular camera encoding; a creative look changes the style.
  • LOG: a camera tone encoding intended for later grading. HDR: a wider brightness range such as PQ or HLG; Reccut previews imported HDR tone-mapped to SDR.
  • Codec: how picture or sound is encoded. Container: the wrapper holding it, such as MP4 or MOV.
  • Bitrate: data used per second; it affects file size and quality. Frame rate: frames shown per second.
  • Optimized media: a generated working copy for easier playback. Keep originals available for future use.
  • Burned-in captions: text rendered permanently into picture. Embedded captions: a separate selectable stream inside a file.

Sound and AI

  • dB: a relative level change. dBFS: a digital audio meter where 0 is the maximum; clipping occurs above it.
  • Compressor: reduces differences between loud and quiet portions. Limiter: catches peaks above its limit.
  • Provider/endpoint: the service and API base URL receiving model requests.
  • Model ID: the exact identifier accepted by that server. Tool calling: the model's ability to request Reccut actions instead of only returning text.
  • Vision model: accepts images as input. Local model: runs on a separately installed server on your computer or network.
  • Processor/plugin: a tool that can run an operation on media, separate from the chat model itself.

Every Audio Effect and Its Controls

Open Audio Effects in the browser and apply an effect to a selected clip. Its controls appear in the Audio inspector. These names, descriptions, ranges and defaults come from the application's effect definitions. Effects process from top to bottom; reorder or disable one to compare. Equalizer

10 band graphic equalizer.

  • 31: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.
  • 62: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.
  • 125: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.
  • 250: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.
  • 500: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.
  • 1k: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.
  • 2k: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.
  • 4k: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.
  • 8k: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.
  • 16k: -18–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.

High Pass

Removes rumble, wind and handling noise below the frequency.

  • Frequency: 20–1000 Hz. Default: 80 Hz.

Low Pass

Removes everything above the frequency. Makes the sound darker or muffled.

  • Frequency: 200–20000 Hz. Default: 8000 Hz.
  • Resonance: 0.3–8. Default: 0.707.

Band Pass

Keeps only a band of frequencies, like a phone or a small speaker.

  • Low cut: 50–2000 Hz. Default: 300 Hz.
  • High cut: 800–16000 Hz. Default: 3400 Hz.

Hum Removal

Removes electrical hum at 50 Hz (Europe) or 60 Hz (America) and its overtones.

  • Frequency: 50–60 Hz. Default: 50 Hz.
  • Overtones: 1–8. Default: 4.

Noise Reduction

Learns the steady background noise (fans, air conditioning, hiss) and removes it.

  • Amount: 0–1 %. Default: 0.7 %.
  • Keep: -40–0 dB. Default: -24 dB.

Noise Gate

Turns the sound down in the pauses, when it is below the threshold.

  • Threshold: -80–0 dB. Default: -45 dB.
  • Reduction: 0–80 dB. Default: 30 dB.
  • Attack: 0.1–50 ms. Default: 2 ms.
  • Release: 10–1000 ms. Default: 150 ms.

Compressor

Evens out loud and quiet parts so the voice sits steady.

  • Threshold: -60–0 dB. Default: -20 dB.
  • Ratio: 1–20 :1. Default: 3 :1.
  • Attack: 0.1–100 ms. Default: 8 ms.
  • Release: 10–1000 ms. Default: 120 ms.
  • Makeup: 0–24 dB. Default: 4 dB.

Limiter

Stops peaks from going over the ceiling, so nothing distorts.

  • Ceiling: -12–0 dB. Default: -1 dB.
  • Release: 10–500 ms. Default: 60 ms.
  • Input gain: 0–18 dB. Default: 0 dB.

De-esser

Softens sharp S and T sounds.

  • Frequency: 3000–10000 Hz. Default: 6000 Hz.
  • Threshold: -50–0 dB. Default: -28 dB.
  • Max reduction: 0–24 dB. Default: 10 dB.

Auto Level

Turns quiet speech up and loud speech down to an even level.

  • Target level: -36–-6 dB. Default: -18 dB.
  • Max boost: 0–30 dB. Default: 15 dB.
  • Speed: 0.1–1 %. Default: 0.5 %.

Gain

Turns the clip up or down before the other effects.

  • Gain: -30–30 dB. Default: 6 dB.

Channels

Choose which recorded channel to use, for example when a microphone was only on the left or right side, or swap the two sides.

  • Use: 0–4. Default: 0.

Pan and Width

Pan left or right, make the stereo wider or narrower, or turn it into mono.

  • Pan: -1–1. Default: 0.
  • Width: 0–2 %. Default: 1 %.

Echo

Repeats the sound, like an echo in a canyon.

  • Time: 20–1500 ms. Default: 320 ms.
  • Feedback: 0–0.95 %. Default: 0.4 %.
  • Mix: 0–1 %. Default: 0.35 %.
  • Tone: 500–16000 Hz. Default: 5000 Hz.
  • Ping pong: 0–1. Default: 0.

Reverb

Puts the sound in a room, from a small room to a cathedral.

  • Room size: 0–1 %. Default: 0.6 %.
  • Damping: 0–1 %. Default: 0.4 %.
  • Width: 0–1 %. Default: 1 %.
  • Mix: 0–1 %. Default: 0.3 %.
  • Pre-delay: 0–200 ms. Default: 15 ms.

Chorus

Thickens the sound with slightly detuned copies.

  • Rate: 0.05–5 Hz. Default: 0.8 Hz.
  • Depth: 0–1 %. Default: 0.5 %.
  • Mix: 0–1 %. Default: 0.5 %.

Flanger

A sweeping jet plane sound.

  • Rate: 0.05–5 Hz. Default: 0.25 Hz.
  • Depth: 0–1 %. Default: 0.7 %.
  • Mix: 0–1 %. Default: 0.5 %.
  • Feedback: 0–0.9 %. Default: 0.5 %.

Tremolo

The volume pulses up and down.

  • Rate: 0.5–20 Hz. Default: 6 Hz.
  • Depth: 0–1 %. Default: 0.6 %.

Distortion

Overdrive, fuzz or a broken speaker.

  • Drive: 0–48 dB. Default: 18 dB.
  • Character: 0–2. Default: 0.
  • Tone: 500–16000 Hz. Default: 6000 Hz.
  • Mix: 0–1 %. Default: 1 %.
  • Output: -30–6 dB. Default: -6 dB.

Bitcrusher

Fewer bits and a lower sample rate, like old games and samplers.

  • Bits: 2–16. Default: 8.
  • Sample rate: 1000–48000 Hz. Default: 11025 Hz.
  • Mix: 0–1 %. Default: 1 %.

Lo-fi Tape

Old tape or vinyl: wobble, hiss, crackle and a dull top end.

  • Wobble: 0–1 %. Default: 0.4 %.
  • Hiss: 0–1 %. Default: 0.25 %.
  • Crackle: 0–1 %. Default: 0.2 %.
  • Tone: 1000–16000 Hz. Default: 5000 Hz.
  • Saturation: 0–1 %. Default: 0.3 %.

Robot

Ring modulation for robot and sci-fi voices.

  • Frequency: 20–2000 Hz. Default: 90 Hz.
  • Mix: 0–1 %. Default: 0.8 %.

Pitch

Lower the voice for a deeper sound, or raise it, without changing the speaking speed or clip length.

  • Pitch: -12–12 st. Default: 0 st.

Voice Character

A deeper, bigger and closer voice in one effect: depth in the low end, presence, density and warmth. Sounds natural, unlike strong pitch.

  • Depth: 0–1 %. Default: 0.5 %.
  • Presence: 0–1 %. Default: 0.3 %.
  • Density: 0–1 %. Default: 0.4 %.
  • Warmth: 0–1 %. Default: 0.2 %.
  • Pitch: -3–3 st. Default: 0 st.

Video Effects, Keys and Film Looks Reference

What each visual effect does and where it fits in the image-processing stack.

On this pageClip effectsOrdered stack effectsMatte and edge controlsFilm looks

Clip effects

Apply these from the Effects browser and adjust them in the Effects inspector. Classic clip-effect amounts can have keyframes. Border colour is a separate control.

  • Blur: Softens fine detail. On a clip it affects that source; an effect clip can affect the picture below it. Range: 0–40px.
  • Vignette: Darkens the picture towards the corners to draw attention to the centre. Range: 0–100%.
  • Sepia: Blends a warm brown tint into the image for an aged look. Range: 0–100%.
  • Black & White: Removes colour. Use Film Looks for specific black-and-white tonal responses. Range: 0–100%.
  • Invert: Reverses the colour values for a negative-like result. Range: 0–100%.
  • Hue Shift: Rotates colours around the colour wheel; large changes also alter skin and brand colours. Range: -180–180°.
  • Rounded Corners: Rounds the source corners. A clip underneath becomes visible around the corners. Range: 0–50%.
  • Drop Shadow: Amount up to 500%, with color, softness, opacity and X/Y position controls. Adds a shadow following the visible source alpha after keying, cropping and masks. Clips underneath remain visible through transparent areas. Range: 0–500%.
  • Border: Draws an edge around the source. Set its colour in the Effects inspector. Range: 0–40px.

Ordered stack effects

  • Solid color: Fills the source with a solid colour. Strength blends with the source; Preserve source alpha keeps its existing transparent areas.
  • Gradient ramp: Fills the source with a linear, radial or freeform gradient. Add and drag colour stops or points, and adjust each freeform point’s spread. Preserve source alpha keeps existing transparent areas.
  • Digital noise: Adds digital monochrome or coloured static. This is independent pixel noise; use Film grain for organic film texture.
  • Film grain: Adds irregular, correlated film grain with a luminance-dependent response. Includes 35mm, 16mm and aged 8mm-inspired presets; optional dust, scratches, gate weave and exposure flicker are under Advanced.
  • Digital glitch: Creates animated horizontal band displacement and RGB separation. Control Amount, Bands, RGB split, Speed and Seed.
  • VHS / analog tape: Softens the full image with separate luma/chroma filtering, color bleed, low-bandwidth tape detail, static, head-switching noise and intermittent tracking faults. SP, LP and EP-inspired presets set the amount of wear.
  • RGB split: Separates the red and blue channels in opposite directions. Separation and Angle control the coloured edge fringes.
  • Chromatic aberration: Simulates radial lens fringing with subpixel channel separation that increases toward the edges. Amount, Edge falloff and Lens center control the optics; the center stays aligned.
  • Scanlines: Darkens alternating horizontal bands for a monitor or CRT look. Amount controls darkness; Spacing controls line distance.
  • Glow / bloom: Screens a blurred copy over the picture for soft bloom. Amount controls strength and Radius controls the spread.
  • Color key: Removes pixels similar to Screen color. Similarity widens the keyed range, Softness smooths the threshold, and Despill reduces coloured edges.
  • Background reference key: Compares the source with a captured clean background. Use a locked camera and a reference with the subject absent.
  • Luma key: Keys by brightness rather than colour, useful for bright or dark backgrounds. Invert reverses the retained side.
  • Blur: Softens fine detail. On a clip it affects that source; an effect clip can affect the picture below it.
  • Saturation: Changes colour intensity. A neutral stack Amount of 1 leaves saturation unchanged.
  • Contrast: Changes separation between dark and light values. A neutral stack Amount of 1 leaves contrast unchanged.
  • Opacity: Changes source transparency. A stack Amount of 1 is fully opaque.

Stack effects process from top to bottom, before the existing colour correction and classic clip effects. Reorder, duplicate, disable or remove instances in the inspector. Stack parameters are static in this version. For recordings, choose Camera or Screen first.

Click × beside an effect name to remove it. Adjusted effects show a warning with Don’t show this again. Restore warnings in Settings → General → Projects. Removal is undoable, including all settings and keyframes.

Matte and edge controls

  • Similarity sets how much background is removed. Softness smooths the transition around the threshold.
  • Motion blur keeps blurred edges of fast movement translucent and removes the screen colour from them, so they blend with the new background instead of turning grey or green. Start around 0.5.
  • Recover fine detail restores fractional coverage in hair and fine edges. Raise gradually to avoid restoring dark background patches.
  • Preserve source shadows keeps darker areas with the screen’s hue as translucent shadows. Select an unshadowed Screen color sample; Shadow color range sets hue tolerance.
  • Recover edge color reduces screen contamination in translucent edge pixels. Spill reduces the dominant screen colour channel.
  • Matte gamma adjusts fractional coverage without changing fully transparent or fully opaque pixels. Values above 1 strengthen fine detail.
  • Black and white points refine the matte. Black-and-white matte view helps identify holes or leftover background.
  • Edge shrink/expand adjusts the boundary; positive edge values shrink the foreground. Feather softens it.
  • Invert reverses visible and removed areas. View chooses Composite or matte. Keep the black-and-white matte selected to export it for use in another project, as video or with Export Frame. Choose Composite to export the keyed picture instead.

Choose a look in Color and use Strength to blend it with the corrected image. These are artistic simulations of tone and colour, not measured manufacturer profiles. They do not add grain or halation. Camera LUTs are a separate input conversion on the source media.

Black & white film: Kodak Tri-X 400; Ilford HP5 Plus; Kodak T-Max 100; Eastman Double-X 5222; Orthochromatic (The Lighthouse); Wet plate (blue sensitive); Silent film; Film noir; Infrared.

  • WAV (.wav): Uncompressed.
  • FLAC (.flac): Lossless.
  • Opus (OGG) (.ogg): Very efficient.

Presets

  • Standard: MP4 H.264 at full quality. Works for almost anything.
  • YouTube and Web: MP4 H.264 at 1080p with a good balance of quality and size, and sound at -14 LUFS.
  • Smallest File: AV1, a small file with high quality.
  • Apple Devices: HEVC, half the size of H.264.
  • Master: ProRes 422 for further editing in Final Cut Pro.
  • GIF: Animated GIF at 720p, no audio.
  • Audio Only: MP3, AAC, WAV, FLAC or Opus.

Quality and fit

Quality choices: Low, Medium, High, Best, or Custom video bitrate. Higher settings normally use more space. Audio bitrate is separate.

  • Fit: Shows the whole picture, with black bars if needed.
  • Fill: Fills the frame and crops the edges.
  • Stretch: Stretches the picture to the frame.

Example: choose MP4 H.264 for a broadly compatible delivery, or a supported ProRes format for another editor. For the editable timeline and original files, use Package Project.
